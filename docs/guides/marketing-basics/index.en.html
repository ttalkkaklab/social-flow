Marketing words to learn first

A CTA is the door one step forward; a funnel is the corridor those doors are strung along. A lead is someone who hasn't bought yet but has given you a way to talk to them again. Starting from those three, this page explains in plain words the vocabulary that comes up when you count people, count money, and choose who to talk to.

Don't memorize the terms — judge each number by whether it tells you what's broken.

When you're making content, you watch the hook, the CTA, and conversion. When you're reading numbers, you watch impressions, CTR, CVR, and bounce. When you're spending on ads, you watch CAC and ROAS. Everything else can be looked up when it comes up.

1. CTA — the line that asks for the next move

A CTA (call to action) names one specific thing to do right now. A button, a link, the last line of a video, the last line of a Threads post — any of them can be the CTA. Without one, people enjoy the thing and leave.

A good CTA makes clear who does what, right now. "Learn more" doesn't show the next step. "Get a free quote" and "Book this week" do.

Vague CTA

Learn more · Click · Link in bio · Get in touch if you're interested

The reader has to work out what to do next. That's the moment most of them scroll.

Sharp CTA

Get the free checklist · See this week's open slots · Comment "quote"

The hand moves on its own. One exit per screen is usually better than several.

The hook is the entrance; the CTA is the exit. The hook stops the scroll in the first one to three seconds or the first line, and the CTA moves someone one step forward once they've watched. A weak entrance means nobody reaches the exit; no exit means even the people who stopped never turn into customers.

Put one CTA on a video, a post, or a landing page. "Subscribe, like, and click the link too" is three exits, and people take none of them.

2. Funnel — the path to becoming a customer

A funnel is wide at the top and narrow at the bottom. Of 100 people who watch a Short, fewer open the profile, and fewer still book or buy. You look at the funnel to find where people drop out.

Awareness
They know you exist Shorts, Threads, search ads. The CTA is one light step — "read one more line", "open the profile".
Interest
They look a bit closer Profile, landing page, reviews. The CTA is "see the reviews", "see the price range".
Lead
They give you a way back to them Email, messenger, phone number, a consultation request. The CTA is "get the checklist", "tell me when a slot opens".
Conversion
They finish the action you defined Purchase, booking, signup, install. A follow counts too if that's what you decided. The CTA is "book this week".
Retention
They come back Repeat purchase, referral, comments, the next episode. If the funnel is how new customers come in, retention is holding on to the ones already here.

Each stage's CTA asks for the one step that person can take right now. Throw "buy now" at someone seeing you for the first time and most of them are gone.

Where the hole is

Pouring on more ads doesn't move the numbers while the same hole is leaking. Check these three first.

SymptomUsually brokenFix first
Lots of views, no clicks Hook, copy, targeting The first line and first 3 seconds; who it's being shown to
They click and leave immediately The landing page doesn't match the promise Make the copy and the destination say the same thing
They watch a long time but never sign up The CTA is missing or too heavy One exit, and an action one step lighter
Signups but no payments Price, trust, the follow-up Reviews, a price range, one message after the lead comes in

A landing page is the single page someone arrives at after clicking an ad or a CTA. It isn't your whole website. One promise, one CTA.

3. Lead — someone you can talk to again

A lead is someone who hasn't bought yet but has given you a way to talk to them again.

Someone walking past has no name and no number. They see the ad and drift by. The moment they write down an email, a messenger handle, a phone number, or a consultation request — "I'm interested" — they become a lead. Not a customer yet. Not just a spectator either. In between.

Watched a tax Short, tapped "Get the free checklist", typed in an email.
Watched a hair-salon Reel, left a number under "Tell me when a slot opens this week".
Clicked "Get a quote" on the landing page and filled in their industry and contact.

None of the three paid anything, but you now have a reason to reach out again. Make your first offer "buy now" and most people won't. That's why it's "take this checklist" instead. Give something small, get a contact, and you can walk them forward one step at a time by email or message. That list of names is your lead list.

What isn't a lead

Spectators

Views, impressions, people who glanced. No way to reach them again.

Likes and follows

Closer to interest. With no way to contact them, still not a lead.

Customers

Already bought or booked. The step after a lead finishes converting.

Near leads, far leads

Someone who only took the newsletter is still far off; someone who clicked "book a visit this month" is much closer. The first is sometimes called a marketing qualified lead (MQL) and the second, ready for sales, a sales qualified lead (SQL). Early on, two questions matter more than the acronyms.

  • Can you contact them again?
  • How close are they to buying right now?
Don't put follower count and lead count in the same basket. Ten thousand followers can mean twenty contacts, and three hundred followers can mean ten consultations this week. The second one keeps a business alive.

4. Words for counting people

"Someone saw it" is counted in several different units. Before comparing numbers, agree on what was counted.

Impressions

Times it appeared on a screen. Shown to the same person three times, that's 3.

Reach

How many people saw it. In the same situation, reach is 1.

Frequency

How often one person saw it. Too often and people start disliking the ad.

Engagement

Reactions where a hand moved — likes, comments, saves, shares, plays. Distinct from merely being shown.

CTR

Share of viewers who clicked. When it's low, look at the hook, the copy, and the targeting first.

CVR

Share of clickers (or visitors) who converted. High CTR with low CVR points at the landing page, the price, or trust.

Bounce rate

Share who saw that one page and left immediately. It grows when the copy and the destination don't match.

KPI

The number you decided in advance to judge the month by. Don't mix numbers that look good with numbers that keep the business alive.

5. Words for counting money

Organic traffic arrives through search, recommendations, and content without ad spend; paid traffic is bought. You use both. Waiting on organic alone is slow, and paid alone stops the day you switch it off.

CPM

Cost of a thousand impressions. What you watch when buying awareness.

CPC

Cost of one click. What you watch when buying visits.

CPA

Cost of one conversion. What you watch when buying bookings or signups. Awareness → CPM, visits → CPC, bookings → CPA.

CAC

What it cost to bring in one customer. Counting tools and contractors alongside ad spend is the honest version.

LTV

What that customer is worth over the time they stay. LTV has to beat CAC for ads to be survivable.

ROAS · ROI

ROAS is revenue per unit of ad spend; ROI is what's left against everything you put in. A good-looking ROAS can still be a loss once cost of goods comes out.

Without knowing what "one conversion" is worth, you can't weigh CPA against CAC. If a booking is your conversion, write down what an average booking earns you first.

6. Who are you talking to

Target

Who this ad or post gets shown to. The wider the range, the vaguer the sentences.

Segment

A slice of the target. Among Koreans living in Vietnam, say, "parents" versus "small-business owners".

Persona

The representative face of that slice. Drawing one person — "Min-su, mid-30s, on an expat assignment, scared of taxes" — makes the sentences concrete.

UGC

Photos, reviews, and videos made by customers and viewers. For a new channel it often works better than an ad you shot yourself. The source of truth on the definition: What UGC is (Korean).

A/B test

Change one thing and split-test two versions. Only the title, or only the button text. Change five things at once and you won't know which one worked.

Attribution

Which path gets credit for a conversion. Someone saw Threads, watched YouTube, searched, and bought — who gets the credit? Early on, just remember not to look only at the last click.
