1.The whole picture — how it differs from improvised recording

In the improvised flow (the ingest guide), the recording is the storyboard's material. Here it's the other way round: the storyboard is the plan for the recording — what you'll say and what you'll show are designed up front, so the finished quality is even and the edit is precise because it has the script to measure against.

📼 THE LANE THIS GUIDE COVERS

What's described here is the short-form full shoot (9:16, filmed in one pass). YouTube long-form (16:9) also writes the script first, but it mixes filmed scenes with generated ones in a single episode, so you film each scene separately and save it under the filename the script assigns (footage/s2-install.mp4 and so on). There's no alignment step — the filename is what pairs a clip with its scene — so section 4's "film it in one pass" and section 5's alignment drift report don't apply to long-form. The filming rules and the layout are nearly the same for both lanes; long-form just films in landscape.

2.Setup (once)

The prerequisites are the same as the improvised flow — whisper.cpp, the STT model, Screen Recording and Microphone permission, ffmpeg. For how to check each one, follow section 2 of the ingest guide as is. You also need a channel (/social-flow:channel add) already in place.

✓ INSTALL ONCE, DONE

whisper.cpp, the model, and the Screen Recording / Microphone permissions are a one-time setup. If you've already done an improvised recording, go straight to section 3.

3.Step 1 — the storyboard and shooting script

YOU

Ask for a storyboard in shooting mode

/social-flow:storyboard money "Hometax year-end tax preview" as a shooting script

Say "as a shooting script", "I'll record it myself", or anything else that signals you intend to demonstrate it yourself, and it goes into shooting mode (if it's ambiguous, Claude asks).

CLAUDE

Research → scene design → writing the shooting script

  • Research and fact-checking work exactly as in a normal storyboard — a time-sensitive number only reaches the script after clearing two independent sources.
  • For each scene it designs what to show, what to say, the transition cue, and the target length, and writes it up as storyboard/script.md, the shooting script. No scene images are generated — the picture comes from your filming.
  • Target scene length is 8–20 seconds — the number of sentences is worked backwards from speaking rate (roughly 5–6 Korean characters a second).
YOU

Approve the script (approval gate 1)

You see the scene count, the cover title, and the key numbers with their sources, and pick [approve / request changes / hold]. Nothing about filming is shown before you approve. Once approved, the next command is handed to you:

/social-flow:ingest money record "Hometax year-end tax preview"

4.Step 2 — filming against the script

Only the main display is recorded. Put the script (script.md) on the secondary display and read from it while you talk — that screen won't be in the recording.

Five filming rules (also printed at the top of script.md)

  • At the end of a scene, stop talking for a second, then switch the screen — that silence-plus-transition point becomes the edit cut. This one habit decides how accurate the edit is.
  • Enlarge the fonts and windows of whatever you're demoing (⌘+ in a browser) — it gets scaled down to a 1080px-wide portrait video, so small text disappears.
  • If you fumble, don't stop the recording — say that scene again from the top. The edit uses the last take and throws the earlier ones away.
  • You don't have to recite the sentences exactly — speak naturally, but keep the key numbers and proper nouns word for word (they're the reference values for subtitles and fact-checking).
  • Turn on Do Not Disturb — notification banners on the main screen are captured too. Screens showing account names or emails are designed around in advance, in the script's "screen" field.

When you're done, type "recording done" in the chat. Transcription and timeline generation then run automatically, the same as in the improvised flow (2–3 minutes for a 10-minute recording).

5.Step 3 — alignment, edit, publish

CLAUDE

Script alignment — matching the recording to the storyboard scenes

  • It compares the transcript timeline against the script to find the cut range for each scene (recording/alignment.json). Cuts always land inside silence — nothing gets cut mid-sentence.
  • If you said the same scene several times, the last take wins.
  • Looking at each scene's keyframe, if the demo's focus is one part of the screen it sets up a crop to that area — fitting a whole 5K screen in shrinks the text 4.7× and nobody can read it.
  • Subtitles come from the corrected transcript of what you actually said, not from the script (with numbers and proper nouns in their original form).
YOU

Review the drift report (approval gate 2)

Improvised lines that aren't in the script, missing scenes, reordered scenes, and numbers or proper nouns spoken differently from the script are reported as a table — pick [refilm that part / drop the scene / take it as is] and the alignment is settled.

CLAUDE

The edit build + platform text

/social-flow:produce money "Hometax year-end tax preview"
  • Cut per scene → crop → 9:16 composite → title overlay → burned subtitles → BGM ducking (volume drops automatically under your voice) → outro joined. The build asserts zero audio/video drift.
  • Phone-viewport review, per-platform text (Threads/IG/FB/YT), and the quality gates continue exactly as in normal production.
✗ WHAT YOU SAID ON THE RECORDING IS NOT A SOURCE

The numbers in the script have already cleared cross-verification. If what you said while filming differs from the script, the script is the reference — you decide whether to refilm or correct the subtitle. A new number improvised on camera doesn't reach the video unverified.

From there, the publish approval in /social-flow:publish (approval gate 3) is the same as the normal flow.

6.The layout of the edited video

Three layers composited on a 1080×1920 dark canvas:

y 190–460 Scene title kicker + title (the cover adds the key number)
Recording band
(cut per scene · cropped where needed)
“corrected subtitle of what was said”
y 1380–1560 subtitle band · below it, the platform UI zone
  • Title (top) — the storyboard's scene title, shown for the whole scene. You can tell which stage you're in with the sound off.
  • Recording band (middle) — the scene's demo footage. Either the full screen or the cropped focus area. If the shrink goes past 3×, the build raises a legibility warning.
  • Subtitles (bottom) — the corrected transcript of what you said, burned in at a position that dodges Instagram's action bar and caption zone.

7.Troubleshooting

Symptom / reportWhat to do
Warning: shrink over 3× Ask for a tighter crop, or enlarge the demo app's font and refilm just that scene.
Warning: scene longer than 20 seconds Ask for the scene to be split in two (an extra cut in the alignment), or compress what you say and refilm. Designing scenes small at the script stage is cheaper.
Report: missing scene / reordered scenes Refilm just that scene — run ingest again with the new recording file and only that scene's cut is replaced. No need to refilm everything.
You refilmed but an earlier take was used Ask to see the alignment notes (the take-selection record), then request a fix to that scene's cut range.
Misheard proper nouns · scene boundary problems Those live at the transcript and timeline layer — use the knobs (WHISPER_PROMPT and friends) from section 7 of the ingest guide.
Total length over 90 seconds Tighten the cuts (less padding around each scene) or drop scenes — 35–75 seconds is the recommended range.

The technical contracts (edit.json · alignment.json · the build gates) are detailed in skills/produce/references/screencast-pipeline.md and skills/ingest/references/timeline-schema.md §alignment.json. You only have to describe the symptom.
