# Field placement in the revised storyboard

A scene can establish a rule and a shot can make a specific choice under that rule. The scene request reads both. The table maps the authoring and review fields to the moment when they matter. The [production research](README.md) and [original audit](template-audit.md) give the evidence behind the changes.

| Owner | Fields and examples | How the next stage uses them |
|---|---|---|
| Project and episode | Language, aspect ratio, visual style, output defaults, cast identities, location and prop sheets, music preferences | Reuse across the episode. An image of a character establishes identity. A scene or shot reference determines where that person appears. |
| Episode and sequence | Premise, opening, title, question, payoff, sequence purpose and scene order | Editorial review and playback order. A reason for a choice stays in the board unless it describes a visible or audible result. |
| Scene story | Place, time, event, emotional turn, scene-wide design notes, reveal order and point of attention | The scene header shows these choices before the first shot exists. The video planner retains the intended reveal alongside a shot's optical focus. |
| Scene geography and look | Axis, shared layout and continuity, lighting motivation, camera policy, optional inherited move, depth and lens policies | All shots begin with these constraints. A local light or prop change can coexist with them. A changed axis or an explicit replacement layout carries forward within this scene. |
| Scene media and sound | Opening/ending anchors, cast in the opening, continuous recording, native speakers, ambience, listening perspective, score decision and audience-effect decision | A shot uses the opening anchor only when it owns that instant. A scene owns one generated request and one continuous soundtrack. Independent effects stay on the post track. |
| Shot picture | Subject, scale, crop, viewpoint and camera height, single/two/group arrangement, insert/reaction/cutaway function, composition, layout, eyeline, screen-side changes, local light and state | A close over-the-shoulder reaction can carry three distinct choices. On older boards the existing crop supplies the scale display when the old size field named a category. |
| Shot optics and camera | Actual move or hold, direction, pace, target, landing, sharp subject or planes, focus endpoints, optional focal length, stop and focus distance | The image request uses the planned opening. The video request follows the shot's movement and focus. A recorded listening choice stays independent of the number of sharp subjects. |
| Shot performance and edit | Cast in frame, speaker and delivery, line, physical action, expression, listener, blocking, closing state, local diegetic sound, incoming join and estimated duration | Preserve speech order and language. Performance determines the hold. The chosen media route determines which controls and assets apply. |
| Within a shot | Move or focus trigger, action or reply that changes the performance, dialogue cue, image pin, accent, sound entrance and exit | Events can share a shot. Exact frame numbers are used when authored. Named triggers also work on a model-paced scene. |
| Generation and assembly | Selected operation and capabilities, numbered media slots, actual frame grid, prompt, approval, job receipt, score file, final edit and accepted timing | On a video request the planner compiles from the current board and material inputs. An explicit wording edit belongs to that request. The review page can display the saved matching request. |

## Authoring and delivery fields

| Tool or field family | Owner and scope |
|---|---|
| `story_sequence` | The episode owns its language, title plan (number, type, headline, thumbnail cue and search term), hook type and opening (promise, first frame, title, thumbnail and widening beat). Each sequence owns its title, purpose, question, payoff and scene membership. Revision hashes identify the saved document. |
| `story_scene` | Each scene owns its sequence, number, place, time, event, summary, turn, closing line and opening/closing charge. Its start/end image, speakers, reference speakers and audience-sound decision apply across the scene. |
| `directionDefaults` | The scene may set camera policy and an inherited movement, lens and depth policies, listening perspective, axis, layout continuity, motivated lighting and narrative attention. A new axis or physical layout carries forward only in that scene. |
| `videoDirection` | The scene owns its emotional and action arc, continuous sound, voice intent, one-take choice, motion references, opening positions and scene-relative keyframes. Frame rate provides the timebase for all its shots. A legacy camera note supplies an overridable default move. |
| `story_shot` | Each shot owns its stable ID, scene membership, story summary, duration and reference image. A timed shot owns its start/end frames, incoming transition, framing, action, opening positions, dialogue cues and image pins within the scene. |
| `shot_icons` | The shot owns its render mode, purpose and reason, physical frame size and reason, angle/camera height, arrangement, cut role, camera view and corresponding angle on the background. The view records the subject, optional subject type and IDs, crop, viewpoint, reason and geometric target. |
| Camera, focus and sound | The shot owns focal length, aperture and focus distance. A move has a technique, reason, target, speed and optional trigger. A focus pull has its own technique, reason, endpoints and trigger. Accents have their own cue. Optical depth describes the sharp subject or planes. Listening perspective overrides the scene sound default. |
| Blocking and cut boundaries | The shot owns space, composition, eyeline, coverage bearing, axis crossing, frame mode and closing state. A planned outgoing transition carries its duration, reason and continuity with the next shot. The execution timeline records the incoming cut at the matching boundary. |
| `shot_dialogue` and performance | The line owns its ID, speaker, exact spoken text, displayed text, voiceover kind and delivery. Actions, beats and acting choices belong to their shot. A visible speaker binding describes that speaker relative to the inspected opening image. |
| `story_music` and audience sound | A named cue owns its musical direction or existing asset and reason. A score decision owns its scene span, start/end shot and offset, source offset, fades and gain. Audience sound belongs to the spotted moment and its native or post delivery. |
| `shot_image` | A requested image owns its frame/role, operation, overrides, crop, preserved features, location/prop/cast inputs, budget and resulting file. Start and end images bind to their own shots. |
| `shot_prompt` | This compatibility tool writes shot action, end state, diegetic sound and inspected opening/speaker positions. It can be used to revise these attributes without creating a video request. |
| `video_plan` | Generation owns the scene/request ID, model, operation, tool and arguments, purpose, maximum cost and any request-specific wording edit. A revision identifies the previous request and the change. The saved request, approval and receipt identify what was submitted. |
