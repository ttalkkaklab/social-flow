# Where filmmakers plan framing, movement and focus

26 November 2026 · Social Flow storyboard research

A director and cinematographer work out **how the scene will play**, then design **the shots the audience needs to see it**. A planned shot has a subject, framing and viewpoint. It can also have a camera path and a change of focus. A scene can establish shared rules for any of those choices. How early the crew chooses the precise lens, aperture, marks and timing depends on the shot. A form that comes closest to answering the question at a glance puts the scene in the heading and gives each shot a row for size/angle, movement and notes. [Filmsourcing shooting form](https://artsandsciences.csuohio.edu/sites/default/files/Filmsourcing_Camera_Shot_List_v1.1.pdf) · [Fitchburg State production guide](https://openbooks.fitchburgstate.edu/preproduction/chapter/the-shot-list/) · [First assistant camera occupational standard](https://www.ukstandards.org.uk/en/nos-finder/SKSC22/maintain-required-focus-and-camera-operation-throughout-shoots)

## What belongs at each level

| Choice | What the team can agree for the scene or sequence | What the board or shot list can specify for an individual shot |
|---|---|---|
| Dramatic point of view and coverage | Whose experience leads the scene, what information is held back, which event deserves emphasis, whether the performance can play continuously | Who or what the audience sees at that point, whose reaction earns a cut, which insert the edit needs |
| Shot size and framing | Whether to keep characters together, isolate them gradually or reserve the closest view for a discovery | The subject and opening crop, such as a waist-up two-shot or a close-up of a hand. Record the closing frame too when the composition changes substantially |
| Angle, position and eyeline | Actor positions, the line of action and the screen geography the audience should retain | Camera side, height and direction, who is looking where, what appears over a shoulder, and any deliberate crossing or neutral view |
| Camera movement | A governing choice such as remaining still until someone approaches, or following the protagonist throughout | A hold, pan, track or other move. Specify the cue, subject, direction and landing when these matter. An actor walking toward a stationary camera can also change the framing |
| Depth of field | A visual preference such as keeping the room legible, insulating a character from it, or varying depth between the two halves of a story | The subject or planes that should read at a particular moment. Where focus changes, identify the first subject, the next subject and the cue. Moving focus can leave the camera stationary |
| Lenses, aperture and focus distance | Format, a family of lenses, a tested look or a working stop. A built set, effect or difficult move may require earlier precision | A particular focal length, stop or measured distance when it affects the shot. An ordinary drawing can communicate the intended view before the crew settles on exact values |
| Lighting, blocking and continuity | The motivating light, time of day, actor paths, entrances, exits and geography of important props | Local emphasis on a face or object, the performer's mark in this view, the direction of the eyes or an action to match across the cut |
| Duration and cutting | Pace, where to hold the performance and what alternative coverage the editor needs | The action or line that cues a cut or move. An animatic, effects plate or live music cue can justify precise timing |

The sources address different parts of this table. Shot Lister works from the scene and an overhead to individual camera positions. Celtx documents its shot fields. ScreenSkills describes how the DP and focus puller carry the intention onto the set. The axis diagrams make the dependency between neighboring shots visible. [Shot Lister worked example](https://www.shotlister.com/post/ultimate-guide-how-to-build-a-shot-list) · [Celtx shot controls](https://support.celtx.com/hc/en-us/articles/218530327-Shot-Lists) · [Director of photography](https://www.screenskills.com/skills-checklists/scripted-film-and-tv/camera-department/director-of-photography-skills/) · [Axis and eyeline diagrams](https://learnaboutfilm.com/film-language/sequence/180-degree-rule/)

### Which document answers which question?

- **Floor plan or overhead.** Where can the actors and camera go? One drawing can show the whole scene's positions, paths and candidate views. **Storyboard.** What will the audience see, and in what order? A sustained shot can use several panels to show an entrance, a reframe or the end of a move. [Shot Lister](https://www.shotlister.com/post/ultimate-guide-how-to-build-a-shot-list) · [Toon Boom on camera moves](https://learn.toonboom.com/modules/the-language-of-storyboards/topic/camera-moves1)
- **Shot list.** Which views does the crew need? A setup groups work that can share equipment, lighting or position. Shooting order can differ from story order. One setup can supply material for several points in the edit. [Shot Lister](https://www.shotlister.com/post/ultimate-guide-how-to-build-a-shot-list) · [Adobe practitioner guide](https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/video/discover/shot-list.html)
- **Rehearsal, focus marks and camera report.** Can the crew execute the move, where will focus land, and what did it actually shoot? A take is an attempt to record the shot. A camera report can record scene, take, lens and stop. The board can specify a focus handoff long before the distances are measured. [Focus puller standard](https://www.ukstandards.org.uk/en/nos-finder/SKSC22/maintain-required-focus-and-camera-operation-throughout-shoots) · [Hunter College camera report](https://fm.hunter.cuny.edu/handbook/docs/camera_log.pdf)

## How productions made the decisions

| Production | Shared intent | What the crew did with a particular shot |
|---|---|---|
| *Parasite* | Hong Kyung-pyo and Bong Joon-ho discussed how to stage the families and keep the houses intelligible. | Bong drew a continuous reveal of the house. In rehearsal they checked whether the Steadicam path and lens captured the space and light. In the underground passage, a close view of Ki-taek calling out called for a different camera position. [Hong's illustrated commentary](https://cine21.com/news/view/?mag_id=93175) |
| *Decision to Leave* | Park Chan-wook wanted restraint in the first part. Kim Ji-yong says he shot most of the picture around T4 to retain more of the background. | The camera could move in a beat after a performer turned, as if the observer wanted a closer look. The team discussed which moves to restrict and when curiosity justified one. [Kim in Cine21](https://cine21.com/news/view/?mag_id=100490) · [Park and Kim in British Cinematographer](https://britishcinematographer.co.uk/kim-ji-yong-decision-to-leave/) |
| *The Power of the Dog* | Jane Campion and Ari Wegner developed the visual plan together over a long preparation period. | They drew shots, performed the scenes themselves, photographed positions on the sets under construction and revised the drawings. Scene blocking and individual shot design fed back into each other. [Wegner interview](https://filmmakermagazine.com/112712-dp-ari-wegner-the-power-of-the-dog/) |
| *Oppenheimer* | Hoyte van Hoytema wanted intimate close-ups that retained the world at the edge of the frame. | For the final approach to Oppenheimer's face the team tested different eyelines. The chosen eyeline and increasingly shallow depth worked together over a single push-in. [Final shot breakdown](https://filmmakermagazine.com/123961-interview-hoyte-van-hoytema-oppenheimer/) |
| *Barbie* and *Beau Is Afraid* | For Barbieland, Rodrigo Prieto balanced the miniature look against Greta Gerwig's wish to see the sets behind the performers. | On *Beau Is Afraid* the crew describes opening the lens to put attention on the eyes for a particular shot. A depth preference for a whole world and an aperture choice for a shot are both documented practices. [Prieto](https://www.panavision.com/highlights/highlights-detail/collector's-edition) · [Pogorzelski and Racine](https://www.panavision.com/highlights/highlights-detail/journey-of-extremes) |
| *La La Land* and *1917* | Long passages were designed to feel continuous. | Sandgren needed the same take to accommodate a dancer's full body and a close view. Deakins describes matching camera speed, actor position and lens distance across locations to preserve the impression of a continuous journey. [Sandgren](https://www.panavision.com/highlights/highlights-detail/cinematographer-linus-sandgren-fsf-on-la-la-land) · [Deakins](https://britishcinematographer.co.uk/exclusive-interview-roger-deakins-cbe-bsc-asc-on-1917-bc97/) |

**When the decision is made varies.** Robert Yeoman describes matching a lens to the board's field of view on set, and selecting it in advance when a set must be built. Kim Hyung-koo and Park Hong-yeol describe finding zoom speed and timing with Hong Sang-soo and the actors. Jarin Blaschke went straight to boards on *The Northman*. These interviews support planning at shot resolution while leaving room for different documents and decision dates. [Yeoman](https://cookeoptics.com/news-and-events/wes-anderson-and-robert-yeoman-asc-straight-shooting-imagineers/) · [Kim and Park](https://cine21.com/news/view/?mag_id=86971) · [Blaschke](https://britishcinematographer.co.uk/jarin-blaschke-the-northman/)

**Format changes the workflow.** Toon Boom's animation terminology calls a live-action shot a *scene*. Several panels can belong to it. At Disney, layout artists stage shots and refine the virtual camera again after animation. In observational documentary, *Dina* adopted locked medium and wide views to preserve the subject's surroundings. Kirsten Johnson describes finding coverage as events unfold. A BBC orchestral production instead used a camera script aligned to the music, with individual camera assignments and cues. [Toon Boom terminology](https://docs.toonboom.com/help/storyboard-pro-22/storyboard/getting-started/panel.html) · [Disney layout](https://www.disneyanimation.com/process/layout/) · [Dina](https://www.sundance.org/blogs/dina-comes-to-sundance-shes-a-movie-star-and-we-are-her-paparazzi-3/) · [Johnson](https://filmmakermagazine.com/97135-what-a-documentary-dp-does-kirsten-johnson-on-cameraperson/) · [BBC production paper](https://downloads.bbc.co.uk/rd/pubs/whp/whp-pdf-files/WHP123.pdf)

**The depth rule deserves a narrower interpretation.** Social Flow's directing guide starts from the number of things the viewer must read. It prescribes shallow depth for one and deep focus for two or more, with a reason for departures. This is a project heuristic. Two faces at the same distance can both be sharp in a shallow-focus image. A lone face may need a readable background, and two subjects at different distances may need to become sharp successively. For authoring, ask *which plane or subject should be sharp, when, and what should remain readable behind it?* Scene-level depth or lens intent could be shown once and individual shot choices called out where they change. The current scene-default schema has no dedicated depth or lens field. **Example for a Social Flow scene.** A daughter opens an envelope and discovers her mother already knows what is inside. The scene establishes that they sit opposite each other, the window is to the daughter's left and the camera stays on the door side of the table. The following shot choices are illustrative.

| Shot | What the audience needs | What goes on the card |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Both people and the unopened envelope in the same space | Stationary medium two-shot. Keep the table and both faces readable. |
| 2 | First the letter, then the mother's reaction | Letter in the foreground, mother beyond it. Keep the camera still. When the daughter stops reading, pull focus from the letter to her mother's eyes. Two depicted moments can share one shot. |
| 3 | The daughter realizes her mother has been waiting | Close-up of the daughter from the established camera side. Hold until she looks up. |

Publication count measures the breadth of the review. Several people discuss the same film and a publisher may contribute multiple resources. For claims about planning, production forms, formal role descriptions, original boards and participants' accounts carry the most weight. Classroom exercises illustrate a method. They cannot establish how often a working crew uses it.

The sample favors publicly documented scripted productions, especially English-language features and cinematography interviews. It cannot establish an industry percentage for preselecting a lens, fixing a stop or storyboarding every shot. The repository comparison covers the current authoring contract and review template.
