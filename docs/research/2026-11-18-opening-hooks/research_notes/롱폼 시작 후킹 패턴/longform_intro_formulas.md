# Long-form YouTube intro formulas (first 15–60 s) and packaging–intro alignment

Research date: 2026-11-18. Scope: horizontal long-form YouTube (not Shorts).
Source quality note: the one primary practitioner document reached in full is the leaked
"How to Succeed in MrBeast Production" PDF (2024), read as extracted text. YouTube Help's
retention page was read directly. Paddy Galloway, Colin and Samir, Ed Lawrence and vidIQ material
came through transcript-summary pages (rosetta.to, thetranscriptdesk). Those pages are
machine-assisted transcripts ("edited" in the Transcript Desk URL means lightly cleaned), so
quotes may be paraphrased by the transcriber. **Transcript Desk labels many lines jointly
"Colin and Samir / Paddy Galloway"; attribution to Galloway below was assigned by context.**
Only Happy News is an uncited editorial page: lines it gives Galloway may be the article's
wording. **[snippet]** after a link = the claim came from a search-result summary and that page
itself was not opened; treat as unverified. 1of10's blog lists creator lines (MrBeast, Ludwig, Ali Abdaal,

## Q1. Packaging alignment — how the intro must tie to the title/thumbnail promise

### Takeaway
Every primary or near-primary source agrees on one rule: the intro's first job is to prove the
click was right ("confirm the click"), then raise a new or higher curiosity than the packaging did.
YouTube's own analytics defines the 30-second "Intro" metric in exactly these terms. MrBeast's
production document makes knowing the title/thumbnail a precondition for scripting the opening
and blames a mismatch for first-minute loss.

### Cited Findings

**Official platform definition**
- YouTube Studio's "Intro" key moment = "what percentage of your audience still watched your video after the first 30 seconds." A high intro percentage could mean "The content in the first 30 seconds matched the viewer's expectation of the video's thumbnail and title." Suggested fixes: "Consider changing your video thumbnail and title to better reflect your video content" and "Modify the first 30 seconds of your video and experiment with different styles." The page gives no numeric "above typical" threshold. — [YouTube Help: Measure key moments for audience retention](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415?hl=en)
- Third-party sites claim videos with ≥50% still watching at 30 s appear in an "above typical intros" list; not found on the official page — [Search-result snippets via subscribr.ai](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/youtube-key-moments-audience-retention) [snippet] (unverified)

**MrBeast production document (leaked 2024; authenticity confirmed to Passionfruit by two anonymous ex-producers, not officially confirmed)** — [Passionfruit report](https://passionfru.it/?p=81151) [snippet]; [PDF copy](https://prog.sijanec.eu/dir/How-To-Succeed-At-MrBeast-Production.pdf); [Simon Willison notes](https://simonwillison.net/2024/Sep/15/how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/) [snippet]
- "The title and thumbnail on the videos you will be producing set the expectations for the viewer for your video." Bouncy-castle example: title "World's Largest Bouncy Castle", thumbnail a giant yellow castle beside huge buildings; video shows a red, smaller castle in an empty field → "You'd feel like you were lied to and click off… THIS IS WHY YOU MUST KNOW THE TITLE AND THUMBNAILS OF THE VIDEOS YOU ARE MAKING! How can you know how to start your video if you don't even know what expectations the viewers have of you?" — [PDF p.6](https://prog.sijanec.eu/dir/How-To-Succeed-At-MrBeast-Production.pdf)
- "As with almost every video on Youtube, the first minute has the most loss." On one video 60 M clicked and 21 M left in the first minute ("which surprisingly compared to other channels is above average" — i.e. this loss was *better* than most channels manage). Listed causes of a worse start: "the start of the video wasn't well-lit, I didn't match the expectations of the clickbait, I didn't pre plan what I'd say, and we didn't front load some interesting stuff." "The first minute of each video is the most important minute of each video." — [PDF p.7–8](https://prog.sijanec.eu/dir/How-To-Succeed-At-MrBeast-Production.pdf)
- Packaging examples in the same doc: "I Spent 50 Hours In My Front Yard" (lame) vs "I Spent 50 Hours In Ketchup"; "I Don't Like Bananas" vs "Bananas Are The Worst Food On Earth" ("the more extreme the better"). — [PDF p.6](https://prog.sijanec.eu/dir/How-To-Succeed-At-MrBeast-Production.pdf)

**Paddy Galloway (consultant to MrBeast and others), from a Colin and Samir conversation transcript — speaker assigned by context (see header)**
- "confirm the click—you clicked, so the video must match the thumbnail"; "immediately deliver on the promise"; "make your viewer feel like they made the right decision and not stupid for clicking." — [The New Rules of YouTube (2025), transcript](https://thetranscriptdesk.substack.com/p/the-new-rules-of-youtube-2025-edited)
- "If you make a promise in your title, thumbnail, or intro, can you deliver visually or verbally?" — [same](https://thetranscriptdesk.substack.com/p/the-new-rules-of-youtube-2025-edited)
- "Thumbnails and titles don't necessarily have to match exactly"; "introduce a new hook—something unexpected from the thumbnail"; "introduce a new question if the thumbnail posed one before, so the viewer stays until the end." — [same](https://thetranscriptdesk.substack.com/p/the-new-rules-of-youtube-2025-edited)
- "the viewer anchors the video badly and forms a negative impression early" (on weak openings); recounting MrBeast: "if the first 30 seconds was strong, he would almost always find the rest of the content great." — [same](https://thetranscriptdesk.substack.com/p/the-new-rules-of-youtube-2025-edited)
- Jimmy's (MrBeast's) packaging calibration as quoted by Galloway: "Pull your packaging to the most exaggerated version of what it is" (then pull back to what you are comfortable with). — [same](https://thetranscriptdesk.substack.com/p/the-new-rules-of-youtube-2025-edited); [Podwise summary of Galloway masterclass](https://podwise.ai/episodes/8899459) [snippet]
- Attributed to Galloway by an uncited article: "you make title thumbnails that people click, and videos that people watch. It is 50/50." Deliver on the click "within the first 5 to 10 seconds." — [Only Happy News, 2026-08-05 (no primary citation given)](https://onlyhappynews.com/happy-studio/mr-beasts-secret-youtube-consultant-has-worked-on-50-billion-views-here-are-his-actual-rules/)
- Window conflict: one podcast summary says 45 s is critical, the written framework says 5–10 s, the MrBeast anecdote says 30 s. — [Podwise](https://podwise.ai/episodes/8899459) [snippet]; [Only Happy News](https://onlyhappynews.com/happy-studio/mr-beasts-secret-youtube-consultant-has-worked-on-50-billion-views-here-are-his-actual-rules/)

**Colin and Samir**
- Samir: the first seven seconds are "about confirming the click" — check the video matches the title/thumbnail and does not feel like clickbait. If the thumbnail shows a person, that person should appear early; Mark Rober example of a recipe thumbnail with an exploding pizza as a mismatch case. — [The YouTube Playbook in 37 Minutes (rosetta transcript)](https://rosetta.to/u/colinandsamir/the-youtube-playbook-in-37-minutes)
- Colin dislikes "hook" and prefers "promise" — a clear statement of what the video is about. — [same](https://rosetta.to/u/colinandsamir/the-youtube-playbook-in-37-minutes)
- Cleo Abram (guest): the title/thumbnail should create a curiosity gap rather than contain the whole video. — [same](https://rosetta.to/u/colinandsamir/the-youtube-playbook-in-37-minutes)

**Ed Lawrence (YouTube coach)**
- Treat the intro as an extension of the thumbnail and title; restate the promise in the first lines. Matching expectations "stops a viewer from leaving, but it's not enough to make a viewer want to stay"; "you need to write an intro that spikes curiosity even higher than the thumbnail and title did using a hook." — [YouTube's Hard, Until Businesses Write Intros Like This (rosetta transcript)](https://rosetta.to/u/ed-lawrence/youtube-s-hard-until-businesses-write-intros-like-this)

**Veritasium / Derek Muller, "Clickbait is Unreasonably Effective" (2021, orig. "We Need To Talk About Clickbait")**
- Distinguishes Type 1 packaging that accurately signals the video ("Legitbait") from Type 2 that deceives/exaggerates ("Clicktrap"); Type 2 invites backlash. — [GIGAZINE summary](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20210830-clickbait-effective)
- Case: "Strange Applications of the Magnus Effect" → retitled "Backspin Basketball Flies Off Dam" with a clearer thumbnail, then steady growth; asteroid video passed 10 M after retitle to "These Are The Asteroids To Worry About" plus new thumbnail; audience survey preferred "The Biggest Myth in Education" over "You Are NOT a Visual Learner". — [GIGAZINE](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20210830-clickbait-effective)
- Credits/date metadata. — [UvA video listing](https://video.uva.nl/media/Clickbait+is+Unreasonably+Effective/0_czmelz3f/22054)

**Jake Thomas (Creator Hooks; title specialist)**
- Best titles spark curiosity, fear and/or desire; of ~1,000 broken-down titles, 60% curiosity, 46% desire, 39% fear. Advises writing the title before filming so packaging is built around a clear promise. — [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/creator-hooks-jake-thomas-code-on-clickable-youtube-titles/) [snippet]; [Vidpros](https://vidpros.com/insiders/jake-thomas-youtube-title-strategy/) [snippet]

**Packaging-alignment patterns (catalog entries P1–P4)**
- **P1. Confirm the click.** Mech: the first image/line shows the object the thumbnail showed and restates the title's promise, so the viewer stops doubting the click. T (researcher-written): "[thumbnail object on screen] This is [title claim] — and it's real." Ex: MrBeast bouncy-castle counter-example; Samir's 0–7 s rule; Mark Rober exploding-pizza mismatch case (sources above). Works: any packaged video. Backfires: when it only restates and adds no new question (Ed Lawrence: stops leaving, does not make viewers stay).
- **P2. Escalate past the packaging / new question.** Mech: once the click is confirmed, add a question or element the thumbnail did not show. Ex: Galloway "introduce a new hook—something unexpected from the thumbnail"; Colin's three tests by 15–30 s; Ed Lawrence's "spikes curiosity even higher" (sources above). Backfires *(inference)*: a new question that replaces rather than extends the packaged one reads as bait-and-switch.
- **P3. Exaggerate-then-pull-back packaging.** Mech: write the most extreme true framing, then pull back to what the video can honestly show, so the intro can deliver it. Ex: Jimmy's framework via Galloway; "I Don't Like Bananas" → "Bananas Are The Worst Food On Earth" (sources above). Backfires: stopping at the exaggerated version produces the castle mismatch.
- **P4. Legitbait (accurate packaging).** Mech: packaging that briefly and accurately signals the video, so the intro's job is proof, not repair. Ex: Veritasium's Magnus-effect → "Backspin Basketball Flies Off Dam" retitle (source above). Backfires: Type 2 "clicktrap" invites backlash (Muller, per GIGAZINE).

**Benchmarks (secondary, unverified)**
- 1of10: first-30-s retention >70% "solid", >80% "exceptional"; "If you're below 50% in the first 10-15 seconds, your hook isn't working." No source given. — [1of10 blog](https://1of10.com/blog/how-to-hook-viewers-in-the-first-30-seconds-of-a-youtube-video)
- Korean press: long-form loses ~30–50% in the first 30 s ("~고 한다", unsourced). — [EO 매거진, 주힘찬, 2025-12-20](https://eopla.net/magazines/37626)
- MrBeast doc's 21 M/60 M ≈ 35% first-minute loss is described as above average — meaning the video held viewers *better* than most channels. — [PDF p.7](https://prog.sijanec.eu/dir/How-To-Succeed-At-MrBeast-Production.pdf)

### Inferences
- The consensus sequence is **confirm (0–7/10 s) → escalate (new question beyond the packaging, by 15–30 s) → move straight into content**. "Confirm" without "escalate" only removes a reason to leave; it does not add a reason to stay (Ed Lawrence's exact framing).
- Hook–payoff mismatch has no published controlled effect size; the evidence is YouTube's own metric definition, MrBeast's anecdotal retention curves and practitioner reports. Treat "mismatch kills the first 30 s" as well-supported practitioner consensus, not a measured coefficient.
- "Match" means the *visual and verbal object* the thumbnail shows (the yellow castle, the person in the thumbnail), not repeating the title text. Saying the title aloud while showing something else still fails the MrBeast castle test.
- The packaging-before-production rule (MrBeast, Jake Thomas) implies an intro cannot be scripted before the title/thumbnail concept is fixed.

### Gaps
- No quantitative study comparing retention for matched vs. mismatched intros was found.
- Spotter's "How pro YouTubers make intros" post returned 404; 1of10/Spotter thumbnail-alignment posts beyond the hook article were not read.
- Paddy Galloway's own channel videos and the Jon Youshaei interview chapter "MrBeast's intro craft" were found only as chapter titles ([Spotify listing](https://creators.spotify.com/pod/profile/youshaei/episodes/Meet-MrBeasts-Secret-YouTube-Consultant-Paddy-Galloway-Interview-e21i5jo) [snippet]); content not verified.
- Veritasium's own opening 60 s of that video was not transcribed; how Muller opens it is unknown.

## Q2. Intro structures taught by practitioners (structural patterns)

### Takeaway
Practitioners teach a small number of skeletons: MrBeast's time-banded first six minutes,
Colin & Samir's 7/17/30-second ladder, Paddy Galloway's confirm–gap–go, Ali Abdaal's HIVES,
1of10's 0–5/5–15/15–30 blueprint and Ed Lawrence's three-line intro. They disagree on two points:
whether to state a roadmap ("I'll show you X, Y, Z") and whether credibility belongs in the intro.

### Cited Findings

#### S1. MrBeast time bands (first minute → 1–3 → 3–6 → back half)
- **Mech:** treats the retention curve as segments with separate jobs. First minute = most loss, front-loaded; 1–3 min = "transition from hype to execution… Stop telling people what they will be watching and start showing them"; ~3 min re-engagement; 3–6 min = "most exciting and interesting content that is also very simple… lots of quick scene changes"; ~6 min another re-engagement; "If we can get them to watch the first half of the video there's a very high chance they'll watch to the end." — [MrBeast PDF p.7–9](https://prog.sijanec.eu/dir/How-To-Succeed-At-MrBeast-Production.pdf)
- **T (researcher-written):** [0:00 promise shown + front-loaded best material] → [1:00 start doing it] → [3:00 spectacle] → [3–6 simple, fast story] → [6:00 second spectacle].
- **Ex:** 3-minute re-engagement = Karl put in charge of watching Josh in "$10,000 Every Day You Survive Prison". — [PDF p.8](https://prog.sijanec.eu/dir/How-To-Succeed-At-MrBeast-Production.pdf)
- **Works:** challenge/spectacle videos with budget. **Backfires** *(inference)*: when the re-engagement does not fit the story (doc requires it "fits the story").

#### S2. Crazy progression (compress early time)
- **Mech:** cover more story ground in the first three minutes than a "logical filmmaker" would, so the viewer is invested. Ex: a 10-minute woods-survival video covers multiple days in the first 3 minutes instead of day one. — [MrBeast PDF p.8](https://prog.sijanec.eu/dir/How-To-Succeed-At-MrBeast-Production.pdf)
- **T (researcher-written):** "Day 1… [cut] Day 3… [cut] Day 5 — and he's still here."
- **Backfires** *(inference)*: explainers that need sequential logic; skipping steps there breaks comprehension.

#### S3. Early "wow factor" spectacle (inside 30 s)
- **Mech:** an action only this creator can do, placed early, even if it adds nothing to the title/thumbnail. Ex: in "100 days in the circle", the house is brought in by crane 30 seconds into the video; "It's arguably from a data standpoint illogical… but the impression it leaves on the viewer is invaluable." — [MrBeast PDF, "100 days in the circle" passage](https://prog.sijanec.eu/dir/How-To-Succeed-At-MrBeast-Production.pdf)
- **Works:** brand-differentiating moments. **Backfires** *(inference)*: low-budget channels faking scale invite the castle-mismatch problem.

#### S4. Confirm → personal/credibility → new hook ladder (Colin and Samir)
- 0–7 s confirm the click; ~7–17 s "make it personal" (why you are the right person: credentials, costume); by 15–30 s introduce a new hook (Colin's example: promising three tests the host must pass). — [Colin and Samir, YouTube Playbook](https://rosetta.to/u/colinandsamir/the-youtube-playbook-in-37-minutes)
- **T (researcher-written):** "[Thumbnail object on screen.] I've done this for N years / I'm a ___. And to prove it, I'm going to pass three tests — the last one nobody has done."

#### S5. Confirm → curiosity gaps with little context → straight in (Paddy Galloway)
- Three steps: confirm the promise; open curiosity gaps while revealing little context; go straight into content (Podwise chapter summary [snippet]). Uncited article wording attributed to Galloway: "The moment a creator says 'all right, let's get into it,' they have already lost the thread." Transcript (speaker by context): "Some creators overload the intro with context." "flow seamlessly into the first storyline or action point without a big gap." — [Podwise](https://podwise.ai/episodes/8899459) [snippet]; [Only Happy News](https://onlyhappynews.com/happy-studio/mr-beasts-secret-youtube-consultant-has-worked-on-50-billion-views-here-are-his-actual-rules/); [transcript](https://thetranscriptdesk.substack.com/p/the-new-rules-of-youtube-2025-edited)
- "The first seven seconds establish context and lay the foundation"; "We also make a promise that if you stay, you'll get the payoff." — [transcript](https://thetranscriptdesk.substack.com/p/the-new-rules-of-youtube-2025-edited)
- **Works:** challenge, story and explainer videos alike. **Backfires** *(inference)*: topics where the viewer lacks the minimum context to feel the gap (niche jargon topics).

#### S6. Front-load the best value
- Galloway: "the first minute might get 20 or 30% of the attention—frontloading the video with the best value is key." — [transcript](https://thetranscriptdesk.substack.com/p/the-new-rules-of-youtube-2025-edited). MrBeast doc: "front load some interesting stuff for the first minute." — [PDF p.7](https://prog.sijanec.eu/dir/How-To-Succeed-At-MrBeast-Production.pdf)
- **Backfires** *(inference)*: if the front-loaded item *is* the payoff, nothing pulls the viewer forward (see S12 tension).
- **T (researcher-written):** open on the strongest *non-final* moment. **Works** *(inference)*: long videos (Galloway's 20–30% attention share).

#### S7. HIVES — Hook, Intro, Value, End screen (Ali Abdaal)
- Hook = the promise in the first ~10 s; Intro = credibility (who you are, why care); can merge with the hook on simple videos. Example per a summary: Ali cites reading many productivity books over 10 years as credibility. — [Think Media/summify summary](https://summify.io/discover/how-to-easily-script-youtube-videos-to-get-more-views-2rzzRxixzq4) [snippet]; [recapio summary](https://recapio.com/digest/how-to-easily-script-youtube-videos-to-get-more-views-by-think-media) [snippet]; [Ali's Script Template notes](https://q4-garden.pages.dev/sources/Ali's-Script-Template) [snippet]
- Conflict in Ali's own figures: summaries say packaging drives ~80% of success; in one video he says packaging is ~20% of view-count variation. — [same search set; rosetta Ali Abdaal 2026](https://rosetta.to/u/aliabdaal/honest-youtube-advice-for-45-minutes-straight-2026) [snippet]

#### S8. 1of10 30-second blueprint
- 0:00–0:05 attention grab (shock, tease, question or clip); 0:05–0:15 clarify the promise; 0:15–0:30 stakes, context or start the journey; a short branded intro may follow after 5–10 s. Hooks ideally 5–10 s; longer acceptable for 30+ minute videos "if value still arrives quickly." — [1of10](https://1of10.com/blog/how-to-hook-viewers-in-the-first-30-seconds-of-a-youtube-video)

#### S9. Three-line intro + "starting gun" (Ed Lawrence)
- Intros "never over three short lines long"; once you state what the video covers ("I'll show you X, Y, and Z"), move straight to the first point — the setup is a "starting gun"; continuing to talk after it is a mistake. Cites unnamed "studies" that viewers decide in 8 s (unverified). — [Ed Lawrence](https://rosetta.to/u/ed-lawrence/youtube-s-hard-until-businesses-write-intros-like-this)
- **Roadmap tension (tentative):** Ed allows a one-line roadmap as the starting gun; an uncited article attributes to Galloway that "let's get into it" loses the thread. If both hold, the roadmap is at most one line placed immediately before content, not a segment. *(synthesis resting on one uncited quote)*

#### S10. A-to-Z map / "video game map"
- Max Joseph (cited by Colin & Samir): every great story needs an A-to-Z so viewers know the start and end point. Mark Rober uses a "video game map" graphic that zooms out to show remaining challenges. — [Colin and Samir](https://rosetta.to/u/colinandsamir/the-youtube-playbook-in-37-minutes)
- **T (researcher-written):** "There are five levels. Level one is easy. Level five has never been finished."
- **Works:** challenge, build, multi-step experiments. **Backfires** *(inference)*: stories whose surprise depends on the viewer not knowing the shape.

#### S11. Cold open / flash-forward / "action hook"
- Tease the payoff or open mid-moment, then rewind. 1of10 T: "I almost gave up halfway through this challenge…"; vidIQ: open mid-moment then rewind to explain how you got there, best for story-driven/personal videos, ex. "I've lost 43 subscribers." — [1of10](https://1of10.com/blog/how-to-hook-viewers-in-the-first-30-seconds-of-a-youtube-video); [vidIQ 9 Best YouTube Intros](https://rosetta.to/u/vidiq/9-best-youtube-intros-to-boost-watch-time)
- Definition: appears before the title sequence; variations include climax-then-flashback. Weak pattern warned: cold-open teaser → long animated logo → greeting. — [Creator Essentials glossary](https://www.creatoressentials.com/glossary/cold-open/) [snippet]; [Clueso glossary](https://www.clueso.io/glossary/cold-open) [snippet]

#### S12. Teaser montage / best-moment clip first
- 1of10: open with a funny, shocking or emotional clip, then backtrack; best for vlogs and storytelling. — [1of10](https://1of10.com/blog/how-to-hook-viewers-in-the-first-30-seconds-of-a-youtube-video)
- **Contradicted (Korean source):** 주힘찬 lists "이번 화 하이라이트 미리보기" among things never to put in an opening. — [EO Planet, 2026-05-14](https://eopla.net/magazines/42390). His other article lists "결론이나 하이라이트 영상을 처음에 배치" as a common method but prefers starting with content; story-driven videos where spoilers matter should start with content. — [EO 매거진 2025-12-20](https://eopla.net/magazines/37626)
- **Inference:** a montage of later moments without context reads as noise; a single clip that poses a question (cold open, S11) is the version practitioners defend.

#### S13. Big reveal / result first
- Show the outcome, then explain. T: "This is what happens when you code for 24 hours straight." — [1of10](https://1of10.com/blog/how-to-hook-viewers-in-the-first-30-seconds-of-a-youtube-video)
- **Backfires** *(inference)*: if the result is the only reason to watch; pair with "how/what it cost" question (cf. project's episode-anticipation rule).

#### S14. Problem first
- Name the pain, then promise the solution. T: "Most creators fail before they ever hit 1,000 subs. Here's why." — [1of10](https://1of10.com/blog/how-to-hook-viewers-in-the-first-30-seconds-of-a-youtube-video)
- **Works** *(inference)*: how-to and advice searches with a known pain. **Backfires** *(inference)*: entertainment/story packaging, where a lecture-style opening breaks the promised tone.

#### S15. Stakes with consequence
- Clear goal and consequence. T: "I gave myself 24 hours to turn $1 into $1,000. If I fail, I eat a raw egg." Unverified attribution: MrBeast "I'm going to survive 24 hours buried alive. Starting now." — [1of10](https://1of10.com/blog/how-to-hook-viewers-in-the-first-30-seconds-of-a-youtube-video). vidIQ "raise the stakes": numeric or emotional risk, ex. "if this video doesn't get 10,000 views, I'm quitting YouTube." — [vidIQ](https://rosetta.to/u/vidiq/9-best-youtube-intros-to-boost-watch-time)

#### S16. "Exactly what / when / why" promise
- State what the viewer gets, when, and why it is worth their time. Ex: "In the next 60 seconds, I'm going to teach you how to write an intro so good that people actually stay." — [vidIQ](https://rosetta.to/u/vidiq/9-best-youtube-intros-to-boost-watch-time)
- **Works** *(inference)*: tutorials. **Backfires** *(inference)*: a time promise the video then overruns.

#### S17. Target-audience call-out
- Say who the video is for and what problem it solves, blunt but not mean. — [vidIQ](https://rosetta.to/u/vidiq/9-best-youtube-intros-to-boost-watch-time)
- **T (researcher-written):** "If you're a [who] stuck at [where], this is for you." 
- **Backfires** *(inference)*: narrows the audience; wrong for broad-curiosity packaging.

#### S18. But/Therefore causal chaining (Parker & Stone) applied to intros
- Connect beats with "but" or "therefore," never "and then" — from Trey Parker and Matt Stone's 2011 NYU talk aired on MTVU *Stand In*. — [Matt Rickard](https://cdn.matt-rickard.com/but-therefore) [snippet]; [No Film School (Tony Zhou essay)](https://nofilmschool.com/2015/04/tony-zhou-storytelling-video-essay-f-fake-therefore-but-and-then) [snippet]; quote wording from secondary summaries, original clip not checked.
- YouTube application: George Blackman (scriptwriter, quoted by Colin & Samir) — resolve one problem, then set up the next immediately so viewers don't wander. — [Colin and Samir](https://rosetta.to/u/colinandsamir/the-youtube-playbook-in-37-minutes)
- **T (intro, researcher-written):** "We wanted X. **But** Y happened. **Therefore** we tried Z — **but**…" The first "but" lands inside the first 15–30 s.

#### S19. Specific intrigue over generic list
- Attributed to Galloway by an uncited article: contrasts "he discovered something in October of 2017 that changed everything" with "five tips for growing your channel" — the specific line "does more work." — [Only Happy News](https://onlyhappynews.com/happy-studio/mr-beasts-secret-youtube-consultant-has-worked-on-50-billion-views-here-are-his-actual-rules/)
- **T (researcher-written):** "[Person] found something in [month year] that [changed X]." **Works** *(inference)*: documentary/story; **Backfires** *(inference)*: tutorial searches where the viewer wants the list.

#### S20. "Familiar but unexpected"
- Galloway: "your video should be familiar but also unexpected." — [transcript](https://thetranscriptdesk.substack.com/p/the-new-rules-of-youtube-2025-edited)

## Q3. Opening-line techniques that differ from short-form

### Takeaway
Long-form allows a 15–30 s opening with setup, a credibility beat and a stated journey, where
Shorts hooks are 1–3 s. The documented line-level techniques are: cognitive dissonance,
spiky/contrarian thesis, surprising fact, question, curiosity statement, experiment/"I spent X",
stakes, personal emotional stakes, unexpected visual, comparison levels and transformation
promise. Line examples with named creators are mostly unverified attributions.

### Cited Findings
- Length difference: long-form hooks ~15–30 s with room for a short setup; Shorts 1–3 s, "one line at most." — [search snippet aggregating creator-advice guides, e.g. Creator Campfire 4S](https://creator-campfire.beehiiv.com/p/intros-killing-retention) [snippet] (secondary)
- 1of10: hooks 5–10 s ideally; longer for 30+ minute videos. — [1of10](https://1of10.com/blog/how-to-hook-viewers-in-the-first-30-seconds-of-a-youtube-video)

#### L1. Cognitive-dissonance hook (Ed Lawrence)
- **Mech:** list the solutions the viewer has tried, declare they fail, promise what works. **Ex** (before → after, Lyme disease client): long backstory → "Antibiotics, detoxing, and diet changes won't heal you from Lyme disease" + promise of the fix. — [Ed Lawrence](https://rosetta.to/u/ed-lawrence/youtube-s-hard-until-businesses-write-intros-like-this)
- **T (researcher-written, from Ed's example):** "[A], [B] and [C] won't [fix X]. Here's what does." **Backfires** *(inference)*: health/finance claims with no proof on screen (cf. project's promise-and-proof rule).

#### L2. Spiky / counter-intuitive thesis
- vidIQ: contrarian advice that sounds wrong, then evidence; ex. "Stop asking people to subscribe." — [vidIQ](https://rosetta.to/u/vidiq/9-best-youtube-intros-to-boost-watch-time). Unverified attribution: Ali Abdaal "Most productivity hacks are a waste of time. But this one actually works." — [1of10](https://1of10.com/blog/how-to-hook-viewers-in-the-first-30-seconds-of-a-youtube-video)
- Packaging analog: Veritasium "You Are NOT a Visual Learner" / "The Biggest Myth in Education." — [GIGAZINE](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20210830-clickbait-effective); Galloway's "You've been lied to" dodo thumbnail ("desire to uncover a hidden truth"). — [Only Happy News](https://onlyhappynews.com/happy-studio/mr-beasts-secret-youtube-consultant-has-worked-on-50-billion-views-here-are-his-actual-rules/)

#### L3. Surprising fact / statistic
- vidIQ ex. "Did you know that only 6.6% of YouTube channels ever get to 1,000 subscribers?" (figure uncited). Ed Lawrence ex. "90% of people taking antibiotics for Lyme disease never heal" (illustrative). — [vidIQ](https://rosetta.to/u/vidiq/9-best-youtube-intros-to-boost-watch-time); [Ed Lawrence](https://rosetta.to/u/ed-lawrence/youtube-s-hard-until-businesses-write-intros-like-this)
- **Backfires** *(inference)*: unsourced numbers — both example figures above are themselves uncited.

#### L4. Question hook (relatable / unanswered)
- 1of10 T: "Ever wonder why you're not growing on YouTube, even after 100 uploads?"; vidIQ: "If you only had 2 seconds to convince somebody to watch your video, what would you say?"; Ed: ask what the viewer has wondered (do doctors prescribe antibiotics for money?). — [1of10](https://1of10.com/blog/how-to-hook-viewers-in-the-first-30-seconds-of-a-youtube-video); [vidIQ](https://rosetta.to/u/vidiq/9-best-youtube-intros-to-boost-watch-time); [Ed Lawrence](https://rosetta.to/u/ed-lawrence/youtube-s-hard-until-businesses-write-intros-like-this)
- **Backfires** *(inference)*: a yes/no question the viewer answers "no" to.

#### L5. Curiosity statement (withheld detail)
- vidIQ ex. "I uploaded the same video three times and one of them went viral." — [vidIQ](https://rosetta.to/u/vidiq/9-best-youtube-intros-to-boost-watch-time)
- **Works** *(inference)*: when the withheld detail is the video's payoff. **Backfires** *(inference)*: when the answer arrives in the next sentence (gap closed too early) or never.

#### L6. Experiment / "I spent X days/$Y" framing
- MrBeast doc titles: "I Spent 50 Hours In Ketchup", "I Survived…" — [PDF p.6](https://prog.sijanec.eu/dir/How-To-Succeed-At-MrBeast-Production.pdf). 1of10 productivity T: "I tracked every habit for 30 days. Here's what happened." Unverified attribution: Graham Stephan "I made $10,000 from this side hustle in one month. Here's how." — [1of10](https://1of10.com/blog/how-to-hook-viewers-in-the-first-30-seconds-of-a-youtube-video)
- Samir: comparisons across levels of time or investment make the title pose a question. — [Colin and Samir](https://rosetta.to/u/colinandsamir/the-youtube-playbook-in-37-minutes)

#### L7. Personal emotional stakes
- Unverified attribution: Ludwig "This is the hardest challenge I've ever done… and it's ruining my life." — [1of10](https://1of10.com/blog/how-to-hook-viewers-in-the-first-30-seconds-of-a-youtube-video)
- **Works** *(inference)*: personality-led channels with an established host. **Backfires** *(inference)*: unknown hosts; the viewer has no reason yet to care about their feelings.

#### L8. Credibility statement
- Samir's 7–17 s "make it personal" (credentials, costume); Ali Abdaal's intro cites years of reading. — [Colin and Samir](https://rosetta.to/u/colinandsamir/the-youtube-playbook-in-37-minutes); [summify](https://summify.io/discover/how-to-easily-script-youtube-videos-to-get-more-views-2rzzRxixzq4) [snippet]
- **Contradicted:** Ed Lawrence lists "using jargon or credentials" as a common mistake; complex terms make viewers feel less intelligent; speak at a 10-year-old's level. — [Ed Lawrence](https://rosetta.to/u/ed-lawrence/youtube-s-hard-until-businesses-write-intros-like-this)

#### L9. Unexpected visual
- vidIQ: surprising image/effect that the video pays off (cat in a wig); "Don't be random for the sake of being random." — [vidIQ](https://rosetta.to/u/vidiq/9-best-youtube-intros-to-boost-watch-time)
- Korean example of the same move: physical-comedy opening (김지유 × 말왕), see Q5. **Backfires** (vidIQ): an image the video never pays off.

#### L10. Transformation promise
- 1of10 education T: "Let's fix your writing in 30 seconds."; beauty "Don't do this with your skincare routine…" — [1of10](https://1of10.com/blog/how-to-hook-viewers-in-the-first-30-seconds-of-a-youtube-video)
- **Works** *(inference)*: skill/tutorial videos where the result can be shown. **Backfires** *(inference)*: a transformation the video cannot show on screen.

#### L11. Immediate resource CTA (contested)
- 1of10 lists offering a resource up front: "Before we dive in, download the checklist in the description…" — [1of10](https://1of10.com/blog/how-to-hook-viewers-in-the-first-30-seconds-of-a-youtube-video). Conflicts with Galloway/Ed "go straight in" (Q2 S5, S9) and with Korean no-greeting advice (Q5).

### Inferences
- The catalog holds **38 entries: P1–P4 (Q1), S1–S23 (Q2), L1–L11 (Q3)**. Several are one source's name for a move another source also teaches (e.g. S11 cold open ≈ vidIQ action hook; L3 also covers Ed Lawrence's fact hook), so the count of *distinct* moves is lower, roughly 30.
- The long-form-only moves are: credibility beat (L8/S4), stated journey/map (S10), crazy progression (S2), timed re-engagements (S1), and multi-sentence setup like cognitive dissonance (L1). The single-line moves (L3–L5, L9) are shared with Shorts.

### Gaps
- No verified transcripts confirm the creator lines 1of10 attributes to MrBeast, Ludwig, Ali Abdaal and Graham Stephan.
- No measured comparison of these line types on long-form retention exists in the sources found.

## Q4. What to cut from the opening

### Takeaway
Consensus to cut: greetings and "welcome back", channel intros/logo stingers and theme music,
context overload, a "let's get into it" handoff, jargon. Sponsor reads cost viewers wherever they
sit (one conference paper, seen only as a search snippet: ~11% per sponsor read); MrBeast reports a retention "crater" from
old-style reads. No study isolates *up-front* placement specifically.

### Cited Findings
- Greetings: 1of10 cites "Hey guys! Welcome back to my channel..." as the opener to avoid; cut intros or keep under 5 s. — [1of10](https://1of10.com/blog/how-to-hook-viewers-in-the-first-30-seconds-of-a-youtube-video)
- Branding: "save logos and theme music for later"; context before hook is a mistake (lead with tension/curiosity/result). — [1of10](https://1of10.com/blog/how-to-hook-viewers-in-the-first-30-seconds-of-a-youtube-video)
- Stacked opener (cold-open teaser → long animated logo → greeting) as a weak structure. — [Creator Essentials](https://www.creatoressentials.com/glossary/cold-open/) [snippet]
- Context overload: "Some creators overload the intro with context" (transcript, speaker by context); "all right, let's get into it" loses the thread (uncited article wording attributed to Galloway). — [Galloway transcript](https://thetranscriptdesk.substack.com/p/the-new-rules-of-youtube-2025-edited); [Only Happy News](https://onlyhappynews.com/happy-studio/mr-beasts-secret-youtube-consultant-has-worked-on-50-billion-views-here-are-his-actual-rules/)
- Over-explaining, talking past the setup, jargon/credentials, AI-written over-explained intros. — [Ed Lawrence](https://rosetta.to/u/ed-lawrence/youtube-s-hard-until-businesses-write-intros-like-this)
- Sponsor reads: AEA 2025 conference paper estimates an extra ad-roll reduces viewership ~14% and a sponsor read ~11% (preliminary). — [AEA 2025 program](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2025/program/paper/5f44zbss) [snippet]
- MrBeast doc: old-style brand deals left a "crater" in the retention chart (skips and click-offs); integrated deals made "The dip… way less dramatic"; "BRAND DEALS ARE CONTENT!" — [PDF p.33](https://prog.sijanec.eu/dir/How-To-Succeed-At-MrBeast-Production.pdf)
- Agency guidance places sponsor reads at 25–45% of the video or after minute 3–4 (vendor advice, unsourced). — [Creators Agency blog](https://creatorsagency.co/blog/what-makes-a-good-youtube-sponsorship-integration) [snippet]; [Spotter community](https://spottercommunity.beehiiv.com/p/how-to-retain-viewers-during-sponsored-videos) [snippet]
- r/NewTubers community thread "Cut down that intro please" (anecdotal). — [Reddit mirror](https://lr.eu.psf.lt/r/NewTubers/comments/1g88irk/cut_down_that_intro_please) [snippet]
- Trailing fluff: after the final reveal viewers leave within ~30 s, so end there (unnamed speaker on The Try Guys). — [Colin and Samir](https://rosetta.to/u/colinandsamir/the-youtube-playbook-in-37-minutes)

### Inferences
- "Before we start, subscribe" in the opening was not found defended anywhere; vidIQ's own spiky-POV example line is "Stop asking people to subscribe." Treat early subscribe asks as cut-by-consensus (practitioner, not measured).
- Sponsor reads up front: the MrBeast crater and AEA estimate show sponsor reads cost viewers; combined with "first minute has the most loss," placing one inside the first minute stacks two losses. This is an inference — no source measured early vs. mid placement.

### Gaps
- No independent study comparing retention for sponsor reads at 0:30 vs. mid-video.
- No data on long title sequences specifically (only practitioner advice).

## Q5. Korean long-form intro conventions (labelled Korean)

### Takeaway
Korean practitioner writing (EO Studio/EO Planet, 주힘찬 of channel 피보터) repeats the
global rule — no greeting, start with content — and adds an explicit banned list including
highlight previews and previous-episode recaps, which conflicts with the English "teaser clip"
advice. A visual-comedy exception is documented with a named Korean example.

### Cited Findings
- [Korean] 주힘찬 (채널 피보터): "순수하게 피지컬로 웃기는 영상이 아닌 이상, 내용부터 시작하는 게 낫습니다." Exception: a video whose picture is instantly funny ("초등학생이 봐도 직관적으로 그림 자체가 웃긴 영상"). Ex: 천상여자 김지유 (3.7만 구독) couple-yoga video with 말왕 opened on the painful-funny lift; ~90만 views. Claims long-form loses 30–50% in the first 30 s (unsourced). Mentions placing the conclusion or highlight first as a common method; for story videos where spoilers matter, start with content. — [EO 매거진, 2025-12-20](https://eopla.net/magazines/37626)
- [Korean] Same author: opening made "아무 생각 없이" is cause #1 of low views; "영상이 시작되자마자 "안녕하세요" 하는 순간, 시청 지속 시간 그래프가 뚝 떨어진다." Banned list: "\"안녕하세요\", 자기소개, 이번 화 하이라이트 미리보기, 지난 화 다시보기, 채널 로고 인트로는 절대 넣으면 안 된다." Repeats MrBeast's 60 M/21 M first-minute figure. — [EO Planet, 「유튜브 조회수 망하는 이유, 8가지」, 2026-05-14](https://eopla.net/magazines/42390)
- [Korean] A Korean "senior AI long-form" note promotes a movie-like dramatic opening (drama-style cold open for senior-audience AI channels); not read in full. — [lilys.ai note](https://lilys.ai/ko/notes/senior-ai-youtube-20251128/senior-youtube-ai-drama-money-formula) [snippet]

### Inferences
- The Korean banned "highlight preview" and the English "best-moment clip" (S12) can be reconciled: Korean advice targets montage previews of the same episode ("이번 화 하이라이트 미리보기") common in Korean variety-style editing; English advice defends a single question-posing clip. This is an inference.

### Gaps
- No Korean source on 침착맨, 슈카월드, 피식대학 or other large long-form channels' intro structure was found; no Korean retention data beyond unsourced percentages.
- Korean variety-TV conventions (예고 몽타주, 자막 리액션) as they carry into YouTube were not researched.
