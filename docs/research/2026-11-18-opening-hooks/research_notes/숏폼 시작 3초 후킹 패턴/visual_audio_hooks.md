# Visual, editing and sound hooks in the first ~3 seconds of vertical short-form (Shorts, Reels, TikTok)

Researched 2026-11-18. Evidence tags used throughout:
**[M]** measured data (platform or analytics firm), **[P]** platform guidance without published numbers,
**[O]** practitioner opinion, **[L]** lab / academic research (usually not on vertical feeds).
**TEXT** marks a pattern that depends on type over the picture, which this pipeline forbids.
**[U]** = page NOT opened by me; the claim comes from a web-search result summary tied to that URL.
Pages actually opened: Emplifi, three Social Media Today articles (Facebook IQ guide, TikTok sound,
Emplifi coverage), TikTok Creative Center insight, Škripcová (Communication Today), the Kallaway
transcript (sozai), BlitzCut, creatorflow, and the Google Shorts ABCDs one-sheet PDF.
Patterns marked with no example URL have only illustrative (unnamed) examples in their source.

Source quality overview: the only measured study I opened that isolates opening-seconds creative
features in vertical short-form is Emplifi's analysis of brand Facebook Reels (correlational).
Platform documents (Google Shorts ABCDs, TikTok Creative Center, Facebook IQ) give guidance, but
the opened pages carry few numbers. Most "hook type" catalogs are vendor blogs that cite no data.

## 1. First-frame design: what frame 1 shows (catalog)

### Takeaway
Practitioners converge on a first frame that already shows the subject in motion or in an unresolved
state (mid-action, result, problem, odd object, scale, a face mid-reaction), and that reads as a
clear one-sentence promise at thumbnail size with sound off. The only measured support is that a
visible person in the first 3 s and vertical framing correlate with better early retention on
brand Facebook Reels; the rest is guidance or opinion.

### Cited Findings

**Catalog of first-frame patterns** (name — mechanism claimed by source — execution — example — pitfall — tag)

1. **Motion at frame one** — something is already moving when the video starts (liquid pouring, a hand reaching in, a camera push-in). Execution: the motion must be the first second of the topic; "a random zoom or spinning logo moves but promises nothing." Example (illustrative): baker opening on a knife already cutting a cake; fitness coach mid-rep from a low angle. [O] — [creatorflow](https://creatorflow.so/blog/visual-hooks/)
2. **Mid-action opening (in medias res)** — start with the action underway, no setup or greeting; source claims an in-progress action is an open loop the brain wants resolved. Execution: record the intro, then cut the first two seconds of throat-clearing in the edit. Best for cooking, crafts, fitness, process content. [O] — [BlitzCut](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/visual-hooks-instagram-reels)
3. **Face mid-action** — a person caught mid-sentence, mid-laugh, mid-bite, or reacting to a screen. Execution: "cut the first second of most talking clips. Start on the first word or the reaction, not on a face waiting to talk." [O] — [creatorflow](https://creatorflow.so/blog/visual-hooks/)
4. **End result first** — open on the finished product, then cut back to the beginning; payoff before investment. Pitfall: only works if the result is visually impressive; a weak payoff shown up front hurts. Execution: frame the result close, in good light, one clear subject; "a wide, dark shot looks like nothing in a thumbnail." [O] — [BlitzCut](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/visual-hooks-instagram-reels); [creatorflow](https://creatorflow.so/blog/visual-hooks/). A search snippet (not confirmed on an opened page) claims the top 20% of beauty ads by CTR usually show the result in the first two seconds — see Gaps.
5. **Before state (problem at its worst)** — open on the mess, raw clip, bare wall so viewers recognize their own situation. Pitfall: the fix must arrive within the first few seconds; "a long tour of the mess with no change looks like a complaint." Example (illustrative): bare wall with the first picture going up within a second; half the face done. [O] — [creatorflow](https://creatorflow.so/blog/visual-hooks/)
6. **Transformation split** — two states side by side or as a hard cut immediately; the contrast makes the point without explanation. Fitness, interiors, skincare, editing tutorials. [O] — [BlitzCut](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/visual-hooks-instagram-reels). TikTok's Top Ads annotations describe a top-CTR ad's "direct before-and-after narrative" that "keeps the product benefit obvious" (no timing given). [P] — [TikTok Creative Center insight](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/creativecenter/insight)
7. **Unusual / out-of-place object** — an unexpected item that demands explanation (pile of receipts, receipt reaching the floor, skincare product in the fridge beside the milk, oversized object). Pitfall (both sources): the object must connect to the payoff; otherwise it reads as clickbait and "shows as a sharp drop in retention after the first few seconds." [O] — [BlitzCut](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/visual-hooks-instagram-reels); [creatorflow](https://creatorflow.so/blog/visual-hooks/)
8. **Scale contrast** — something unexpectedly large or small next to a reference object; claimed to work without words and survive compression on small screens. Product, food, data, comparisons. [O] — [BlitzCut](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/visual-hooks-instagram-reels)
9. **Mistake frame** — open on something visibly wrong (bad lighting, failed attempt, a mess); interrupts feed polish and opens a loop. Cheap: footage often exists. Avoid for aesthetic/lifestyle and product content (source's matching table). [O] — [BlitzCut](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/visual-hooks-instagram-reels)
10. **Obscured reveal** — subject partly hidden by a hand, focus, off-frame placement or shadow. Execution: keep the reveal under two seconds, "since a longer one spends the attention the format earned." Avoid for how-to and business content. [O] — [BlitzCut](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/visual-hooks-instagram-reels)
11. **Direct eye contact, close crop** — face tighter than comfortable, looking into the lens; "faces are a reliable attention trigger and proximity reads as intimacy." Pitfall: medium-wide framing with a busy background fails. [O] — [BlitzCut](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/visual-hooks-instagram-reels). Note: lab work on static ads finds direct gaze pulls attention to the face and away from the product (see §5).
12. **Tight framing** — Google's Shorts ABCDs (Awareness) list "Use tight framing for ease of viewing" and "Be unavoidable." No numbers on the one-sheet. [P] — [Google Shorts ABCDs one-sheet](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/formarketingshortsabcdsonesheeters.pdf). A Google playbook case "used tight framing in the critical first five seconds to create a hook with rich audio, sound effects, and direct-to-camera dialogue". [P][U] — [Google YouTube marketing playbook](https://business.google.com/in/think/search-and-video/youtube-marketing-success-playbook/)
13. **A visible person within the first 3 s** — Emplifi: a person visible for ≥1 s in the first 3 s → 3-second retention +3.6%, 10-second retention +10.1%, but 30-second retention −2.4%, engagement −2%, replay −4%, reach −7.4%. Confound: 76% of videos without speech also lack a person. [M, correlational] — [Emplifi, 2026-03-19](https://emplifi.io/resources/facebook-reels-data/); sample of 10,110 Facebook Reels from 704 brand Pages per [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/human-speech-and-presence-help-drive-reels-engagement/)
14. **Attractive or unique person** (no mechanism or pitfall given by source beyond what follows) — one of Kallaway's four routes to "scroll stopping visuals"; he says audiences now face a "minimum viable visual requirement" and decide within the first two seconds. [O] — [Kallaway transcript (page attributes to Kallaway; speaker labelled "Speaker A")](https://sozai.app/transcript/create-killer-hook-impossible-skip/index.md)
15. **Recognizable person or brand** in frame 1 (no mechanism or pitfall given by source). [O] — [Kallaway transcript](https://sozai.app/transcript/create-killer-hook-impossible-skip/index.md)
16. **Atypical-for-the-category visual** — visuals that contrast with what the niche normally shows. Execution: compare your opening against top creators in the niche; keep a saved library of videos that stopped your own scroll. [O] — [Kallaway transcript](https://sozai.app/transcript/create-killer-hook-impossible-skip/index.md)
17. **Atypical format / layout** — e.g., Tech Bimbo (Jamie Ganon) frames herself in the lower half and uses the white wall and ceiling above as negative space for visuals; Mark at Open Residency does the same on a podcast set. [O] — [Kallaway transcript](https://sozai.app/transcript/create-killer-hook-impossible-skip/index.md)
18. **Closing letterbox bars** — Sam John Creates uses top and bottom black bars that slowly close to build anticipation. [O] — [Kallaway transcript](https://sozai.app/transcript/create-killer-hook-impossible-skip/index.md)
19. **Green-screen cutout with thick white outline** — a simple effect Kallaway says stopped his scroll; static photos on a green screen, popular clips as B-roll and AI-generated visuals are listed as low-effort ways to meet the visual bar. Kallaway's own Tony-Stark-style glasses overlay "did not work well." [O] — [Kallaway transcript](https://sozai.app/transcript/create-killer-hook-impossible-skip/index.md)
20. **Silent reaction to another clip (Khaby Lame format)** — deadpan face and hand gestures reacting to over-complicated "life hack" videos; understandable without language; Africa Briefing describes it as translating "the visual language of silent film" into social media. [journalism][U] — [Africa Briefing](https://africabriefing.com/how-khaby-lame-became-tiktoks-silent-superstar/); [Yahoo Creators](https://creators.yahoo.com/lifestyle/story/khaby-lame-the-tiktoker-who-went-from-factory-worker-to-billionaire---without-ever-uttering-a-word-061909055.html)
21. **Talent using the product / hands mid-task** — Google: "Lean into the DNA of Shorts and show talent using the product"; "Feature human interactions with the product"; "Focus on your main subject." [P] — [Google Shorts ABCDs one-sheet](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/formarketingshortsabcdsonesheeters.pdf); creatorflow cites "hands mid-task" for an affiliate creator [O] — [creatorflow](https://creatorflow.so/blog/visual-hooks/)
22. **Curiosity opener + close-ups** (no pitfall given) — TikTok's Top Ads annotation: "Leading with curiosity creates momentum," followed by close-ups that carry the viewer through the product promise. [P] — [TikTok Creative Center insight](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/creativecenter/insight)
23. **Familiar everyday scene** (no mechanism or pitfall given) — TikTok annotation of a top ad using soft music and familiar everyday scenes to create a lived-in story (no timing given). [P] — [TikTok Creative Center insight](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/creativecenter/insight)
24. **Product / brand visible early** — Facebook's 2016 study of 850+ ads (Q4 2014–Q4 2015) rated muted first 10 s: only 46% had an easily identifiable brand link and 23% a clear brand message; strongest recall came from ads that showed the product and delivered the message succinctly. [M, 2016 feed video ads] — [Social Media Today on Facebook IQ](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/facebook-releases-new-guide-to-help-advertisers-maximize-video-ad-performan/450818)
25. **Frame 1 is the thumbnail (thumbnail test)** — screenshot frame 1, shrink to ~1/3 phone width, look ~1 s without sound, write one sentence of what the post promises; if you can't, change the frame. Instagram's profile grid shows 3:4 tiles since early 2025, so a centered 1080×1920 frame loses about 240 px top and bottom (creatorflow's own arithmetic; Instagram does not publish it). [O] — [creatorflow](https://creatorflow.so/blog/visual-hooks/)
26. **Vertical full-frame** — Emplifi: vertical vs non-vertical brand Reels: 3-s retention +9.1%, 10-s +23%, 30-s +38.5%, reach +20.9%; ~73% of brand Reels are already vertical. [M, correlational] — [Emplifi](https://emplifi.io/resources/facebook-reels-data/)

### Inferences
- **Perceptual lever per pattern (my mapping, not from sources):** motion onset / looming → 1, 2, 3, 18, 21, 28 (push-in); face / social signal → 3, 11, 12, 13, 14, 20; novelty / expectation violation → 7, 9, 16, 17, 19, 22; scale → 8; contrast between states → 5, 6, 4 (result vs implied process); recognition / familiarity → 15, 23, 24; legibility at thumbnail size → 25, 26, 12; cross-modal agreement → 30, 31, 32.
- Patterns 1–10 share one structure: frame 1 contains an unresolved state (action in progress, result without process, problem without fix, object without reason). The common pitfall in every source is the same: the unresolved state must be the topic itself, or retention drops right after the hook.
- For a pipeline with no type over video, the patterns that carry a promise with no words (1–10, 17–20, 24) are directly usable; generated stills/clips can be authored to start mid-motion and closer than "establishing" framing.
- The Emplifi person-presence result (+10% at 10 s, −2.4% at 30 s, lower reach) suggests a face buys early retention but is not a universal win; it should be chosen per story, not by default.
- "Lo-fi vs polished": the only sourced angle is the mistake frame (polish interruption) and Kallaway's "minimum viable visual requirement" (pressure toward visual distinctiveness). These pull in different directions; no data settles it.

### Gaps
- No real, named examples with URLs for most blitzcut/creatorflow patterns (their examples are illustrative).
- The claim "top 20% of beauty ads by CTR show the result in the first two seconds" appeared only in a search-result summary of an unnamed analysis; not verified.
- No measured comparison of close-up vs wide openings in vertical feeds.
- No source opened on POV-shot openings or ASMR openings; both remain practitioner lore here.

## 2. Editing in the first seconds: cuts, jump cuts, zooms, ramps, whips, match cuts, loops, pattern interrupts

### Takeaway
The best measured finding cuts against lore: on brand Facebook Reels, the pacing of the first three
seconds had roughly no effect on 3-second retention (Emplifi does not define "pacing" or describe its
method, so treat this as weak). Seamless loops on very short videos are the
editing technique with the clearest measured association (replay +18.7%, reach +23.6%). Speed ramps,
whip pans, match cuts and SFX hits have no sourced evidence in this research.

### Cited Findings
- **Pacing of first 3 s ≈ no effect** on 3-second retention; slower pacing slightly positive at 30 s. [M, correlational] — [Emplifi](https://emplifi.io/resources/facebook-reels-data/)
- **27. Seamless loop (videos ≤7 s):** replay rate +18.7%, reach +23.6%, engagement +16.1%; the table also lists +9.4% 3-s retention not mentioned in the prose. [M] — [Emplifi](https://emplifi.io/resources/facebook-reels-data/); replay/engagement figures echoed by [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/human-speech-and-presence-help-drive-reels-engagement/)
- **Loop/rewatch design (Jenny Hoyos):** summaries of her interviews say her average retention exceeds her scroll-through rate because viewers loop; she writes the hook and the last line first; she focuses on the visual nature of the first frame; structure is hook → foreshadowing → smooth transition. Secondhand figures (her average "scroll-through rate" 85% vs a stated 70% average; trimming a second can change retention) come from note-taking aggregator pages. [O, secondhand][U] — [interview summary](https://summ-www.pages.dev/en/summary/meet-the-youtuber-who-solved-shorts); [study notes](https://coconote.app/notes/2e82a5b0-ae17-414b-b607-030f0b523df8)
- **29. Trimmed opening (cut the dead first 1–2 s):** both catalogs tell editors to trim the intro/greeting and start on action or the first word. [O] — [BlitzCut](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/visual-hooks-instagram-reels); [creatorflow](https://creatorflow.so/blog/visual-hooks/)
- **Jump-cut frequency (academic, snippet only):** a 2026 Marketing Trends Congress paper (Dost & Huang) tests seamless vs overlapping jump cuts at low/medium/high frequency against an unedited control; reported that each additional cut uses processing capacity and that the overlapping-cut advantage at low frequency shrinks at higher frequency. The PDF returned 404; sample and effect sizes unseen. [L][U] — [paper URL](https://archives.marketing-trends-congress.com/2026/pages/PDF/paper_professor_DOST_HUANG.pdf)
- **Eye-tracking of attention modes on TikTok:** fast-paced videos produced a "fragmented" mode (shorter fixations, frequent AOI shifts, repeated EDA rises); lifestyle/educational videos a "stable" mode on one or two areas; multi-screen formats a "segmented" mode with rhythmic switching. n=16, 10 videos; abstract says nothing about first seconds. [L] — [Škripcová, Communication Today 2026](https://communicationtoday.sk/watching-tiktok-an-eye-tracking-study-of-visual-attention-paid-to-short-form-videos/)
- **28. Camera push-in / looming:** creatorflow lists a push-in as a frame-one motion [O] — [creatorflow](https://creatorflow.so/blog/visual-hooks/). Lab: simulated looming (object growing) captured attention while receding did not in the same way [L][U] — [Franconeri & Simons 2003 PDF](https://visualcognition.psych.northwestern.edu/publications/FranconeriSimons_Motion.pdf); disputed by Abrams & Christ, who argue motion *onset* is what captures [L][U] — [Abrams & Christ comment](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/the-onset-of-receding-motion-captures-attention-comment-on-franco/); a 2014 review concludes looming and receding attract attention equivalently, with the looming advantage "motoric rather than perceptual" [L][U] — [SciELO review](https://www.scielo.br/j/pn/a/VDmtz96hS9yVTyrtdh6DFMJ/?lang=en)
- **Closing bars as an edit-built anticipation device** (Sam John Creates). [O] — [Kallaway transcript](https://sozai.app/transcript/create-killer-hook-impossible-skip/index.md)

### Inferences
- If Emplifi's pacing result holds, fast cutting is not a substitute for a clear first-frame promise. Škripcová measured gaze and arousal, not retention, and the Dost paper is an unread snippet, so neither is corroborating evidence; they only show that fast formats fragment gaze and (reportedly) that cuts cost processing.
- Starting a move at frame 1 (motion onset, push-in) is the perceptually grounded version of a "zoom punch-in"; the lab debate suggests the *onset* of movement matters more than its direction. Applying lab visual-search results to phone feeds is my inference.
- Loop design is the one editing choice with a measured association; for very short pieces, matching the last frame to the first is cheap and compatible with a no-text pipeline.

### Gaps
- No source opened for speed ramps, whip pans, match cuts, zoom punch-ins (as a measured technique), green-screen/reaction formats beyond Kallaway's anecdote, pattern interrupts as a timed edit, or POV shots. These are widespread practitioner terms, but I have no citable evidence; do not present them as researched.
- No measured cut-count data for the first 3 s of Shorts/TikTok; vendor "cut every 3–4 s" claims cite no study.
- The MrBeast production document was not opened (and is long-form oriented).

## 3. Sound: first sound, SFX, trending audio, silence, voice energy, music drops, sound-off viewing

### Takeaway
Measured data favors speech starting inside the first 3 s (Emplifi: 10-s retention +24.7% vs
music-only on brand Facebook Reels) and platform guidance says "announce yourself with audio." The
widely quoted sound-off figures are 2016 Facebook feed data, not Reels/TikTok; TikTok's "88%" is a
2021 survey attitude, not a sound-on share. I found no primary Meta figure for muted autoplay on Reels.

### Cited Findings
- **30. Speech beginning in the first 3 s** vs music-only: 3-s retention +11.7%, 10-s +24.7%, 30-s +6.6%; sound-on 3-s view ratio +8.8%; engagement +5.6%; but reach −3.3% and replay −11.9%. 61.8% of brand Reels contain no speech. [M, correlational] — [Emplifi](https://emplifi.io/resources/facebook-reels-data/)
- **People-present videos** were 4.3% more likely to be watched with sound on. [M] — [Emplifi](https://emplifi.io/resources/facebook-reels-data/)
- **31. Announce with audio / see & say (Google Shorts ABCDs):** "Announce yourself with audio"; "Coordinate how sight and sound represent your product"; "Use audio to guide the product story"; "Emphasize the product with a see & say approach"; "Keep the pace exciting"; "Design your audio to pave the path to next steps." No numbers on the sheet. [P] — [Google Shorts ABCDs one-sheet](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/formarketingshortsabcdsonesheeters.pdf)
- **TikTok × Kantar "Power of Sound" (reported June 2021):** 88% of TikTok users said sound is essential to the TikTok experience; 73% would "stop and look" at ads with audio (reported as higher than other platforms); sound experienced as "fun" at a 66% higher rate; sample and year of fieldwork not stated. Platform-sponsored survey. [M, survey] — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/tiktok-shares-new-insights-into-the-importance-of-sound-for-marketing-promo/601569). Some secondary sites restate 88% as a sound-on *viewing* share; that wording is not in the report coverage I opened.
- **Sound-off history (feed, 2016):** "as much as 85 percent" of Facebook video views were silent per publishers (LittleThings, Mic ~85%; PopSugar 50–80%; MEC branded clients 85–90%). [M, publisher-reported, 2016][U] — [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/silent-world-facebook-video/); MEC found brand lift and purchase intent unaffected by sound on/off [U] — [Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/most-of-facebooks-8b-daily-video-views-happen-without-sound/419407/)
- **Facebook 2016 guidance:** put key messaging up front and convey it without audio; raters watched the first 10 s muted. [P/M, 2016] — [Social Media Today on Facebook IQ](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/facebook-releases-new-guide-to-help-advertisers-maximize-video-ad-performan/450818)
- **Meta Reels music + voiceover "+15 points"** positive response vs no sound, and a human-presence finding on ~1.2M Reels ads, appear in a "Reels Ads Guide" PDF hosted on a GitHub attachment link of unclear provenance. [M?, provenance unverified][U] — [PDF link](https://github.hscsec.cn/user-attachments/files/15750161/ReelsAdsGuide.pdf)

### Inferences
- The defensible design rule is dual-channel: a first frame that carries the promise muted (because feed audiences and the 2016 data say many arrive silent), plus an audible event at or near frame 1 (because speech in the first 3 s correlates with better retention and Google says "announce yourself with audio").
- For a pipeline that forbids captions, the speech advantage means the first spoken line should start inside the first 3 s, and the picture must agree with it (see Kallaway alignment in §4).
- The speech trade-off (lower replay and reach) suggests music/SFX-led silent-dialogue pieces may loop better; this is inference from correlational data.

### Gaps
- No primary Meta figure for the share of Reels watched muted in 2025–2026. Vendor blogs disagree on whether Reels is sound-on; one claims "over 75% of Reels views happen with sound on" with no source (the search summary did not say which vendor page). The "47% of campaign value in first 3 s" claim traced only to a promotional page. A Meta/Toluna study reportedly found speech + music gave a 2.1× purchase-intent uplift, but only via a secondary summary not tied to a specific opened page.
- No source opened on first-sound-before-first-word, whoosh/record-scratch SFX, deliberate silence, music-drop timing, voice energy/tone, trending vs original audio performance, or ASMR openings. All remain practitioner lore here.
- TikTok's Kantar report body was not readable (blog page returned no content); only the 2021 coverage numbers above.

## 4. On-screen text hooks, and hooking without text

### Takeaway
Practitioners (Kallaway, BlitzCut, Facebook 2016) recommend first-frame text, and Emplifi measured a
small early-retention gain from text overlays on music videos, but text overlays on speech videos
correlated with *lower* 10-s and 30-s retention. For a no-text pipeline, the documented alternative is
visual–spoken alignment plus purely visual promises; the only real no-text example found (Khaby Lame)
is evidence of no speech, not of a measured no-overlay channel.

### Cited Findings
- **Text overlays (Emplifi):** all videos 3-s +5.1%, 10-s +7.6% (table: +7.5%), 30-s "roughly unchanged" (table: +1.8%); music-based videos 3-s +4%, 10-s +6.6%, 30-s +9.8%; **speech-driven videos 10-s −6%, 30-s −4.5%.** [M, correlational] — [Emplifi](https://emplifi.io/resources/facebook-reels-data/)
- **32. Visual–spoken(–text) alignment — Kallaway's three-part hook:** visual hook (what is shown), spoken hook, text hook; all three must point to the same idea, because misalignment makes viewers stop to puzzle out the link and miss the next lines. Misaligned example: a teeth-brushing claim + candy-eating visual + "Gum health secrets revealed". Aligned example: "This is the future of home design" + a home being designed + matching text (~2M views per the speaker). He recommends adding text in the first seconds. Process: sketch the visual hook before editing, like a thumbnail. [O] — [Kallaway transcript](https://sozai.app/transcript/create-killer-hook-impossible-skip/index.md)
- **33. Text on frame 1 (TEXT, not usable here):** "only format that delivers a verbal hook to muted viewers"; 4–7 words, high contrast, clear of UI. [O] — [BlitzCut](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/visual-hooks-instagram-reels).
- **Pictures vs text in ads:** Pieters & Wedel (Journal of Marketing, 2004; 1,363 print ads, 3,600+ people): pictures captured attention regardless of size; text captured attention in proportion to its surface size. Print, not phones. [L, via creatorflow; primary not opened] — [creatorflow](https://creatorflow.so/blog/visual-hooks/)
- **Silent, wordless format at scale:** Khaby Lame built a global audience reacting silently to life-hack clips; coverage attributes cross-cultural reach to language-free deadpan expression. [journalism] — [Africa Briefing](https://africabriefing.com/how-khaby-lame-became-tiktoks-silent-superstar/); [Yahoo Creators](https://creators.yahoo.com/lifestyle/story/khaby-lame-the-tiktoker-who-went-from-factory-worker-to-billionaire---without-ever-uttering-a-word-061909055.html)
- **Google sight-and-sound coordination ("see & say")** as an alternative to supers in the opening; supers are recommended only at the Action stage ("Anchor with supers"). [P] — [Google Shorts ABCDs one-sheet](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/formarketingshortsabcdsonesheeters.pdf)

### Inferences
- The Emplifi split is the most useful measured fact for this pipeline: text helps when there is no voice to carry the promise, and correlates with worse retention when there is one. A voiced opening whose picture matches its first line (Kallaway's alignment, Google's see & say) is the closest documented substitute for a text hook.
- Without text, the muted viewer gets the promise only from the picture, so the first-frame patterns in §1 that read without words (end result, before state, scale, odd object, mid-action) carry the load.

### Gaps
- I found no documented case study or dataset of channels that hook with no text overlays at all, and no A/B test of text vs no-text openings in vertical video. Khaby Lame is a no-speech example; whether his videos carry overlays was not established.

## 5. Perception / eye-tracking: what grabs attention in vertical feeds

### Takeaway
Feed exposure is extremely short (2016 Facebook data: recall after 0.25 s; 1.7 s average per item on
mobile), and lab work shows motion onset/looming and faces capture attention. Direct gaze specifically
pulls attention to the face, away from the product. Almost none of this was measured on vertical
short-form feeds; the transfer is inference.

### Cited Findings
- **0.25 s** exposure is enough for statistically significant recall of mobile feed content (Fors Marsh Group tests for Facebook); people spend **1.7 s** per item on mobile vs 2.5 s on desktop (2016). [M] — [Social Media Today on Facebook IQ](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/facebook-releases-new-guide-to-help-advertisers-maximize-video-ad-performan/450818)
- **13 ms** image-content identification (MIT, 2014; participants knew what to look for). [L, via creatorflow] — [creatorflow](https://creatorflow.so/blog/visual-hooks/)
- **Motion onset captures attention** (Abrams & Christ, Psychological Science 2003, 14:427–432; citation from search summary, primary not opened); **looming** captured attention more than receding (Franconeri & Simons, Perception & Psychophysics 2003). Debate and 2014 synthesis as in §2. [L][U] — [Franconeri & Simons PDF](https://visualcognition.psych.northwestern.edu/publications/FranconeriSimons_Motion.pdf); [SciELO review](https://www.scielo.br/j/pn/a/VDmtz96hS9yVTyrtdh6DFMJ/?lang=en)
- **Gaze direction in ads:** Sajjacholapunt & Ball (2014) eye-tracked banner ads with no face, mutual gaze or averted gaze: averted gaze increased attention to the banner, text and product; mutual gaze drew attention to the face and weakened memory for brand/message; effect especially marked for vertical banners. Adil, Lacoste-Badie & Droulers (JAR 2018, print): face presence with gaze toward the product beat no face / gaze toward viewer on attention, memorization, brand evaluation and purchase intent. Faces and text attract gaze independent of task (Cerf, Frady & Koch 2009, as cited). [L, static ads][U] — [Frontiers in Psychology 2014](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.00166/full); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3941030); [EconPapers (Adil et al.)](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:hal:journl:halshs-01910205)
- **TikTok eye-tracking:** attention concentrated in the central area, very low fixation around it except the upper-central part (Italian agency study, 16 people, four networks). [L, small][U] — [TSW](https://www.tsw.it/en/journal-eng/research-experiences/people-and-social-media-our-research-to-design-more-effective-experiences-and-content/). Three attention modes by content type (n=16). [L] — [Škripcová 2026](https://communicationtoday.sk/watching-tiktok-an-eye-tracking-study-of-visual-attention-paid-to-short-form-videos/)
- **Mobile eye-tracking method caveat:** default fixation algorithms perform poorly on phones; adjusted settings improved "Percent Seen" accuracy by 19%. [L][U] — [Frontiers in Neuroscience 2021 (DOAJ record)](https://doaj.org/article/7c9a6132fc214d4e9b3434157d4f203f)

### Inferences
- Within ~0.25–1.7 s the viewer has only the first frame or two; the first frame's composition (central subject, readable at small size) matters more than anything that happens at second 3.
- Direct-to-camera eye contact is a trade: good for a person-led story, worse when the subject is an object/product — point the character's gaze at the thing the episode is about. (Inference from static-ad studies.)
- Central placement of the subject fits both the TikTok fixation map and UI overlays at the edges.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study measuring face, motion-onset or contrast effects specifically in vertical short-form feeds. High contrast / color salience was not researched.

## 6. Data comparing visual-hook types (ad platforms, analytics firms, A/B tests)

### Takeaway
There is no public, primary dataset ranking visual-hook *types* for short-form. The usable numbers are
metric definitions (hook rate / thumbstop), Emplifi's per-feature correlations, Google's
framework-level ABCD lift, and TikTok's recall-timing claim, which I could not confirm on the page.

### Cited Findings
- **Hook rate / thumbstop:** Motion's glossary treats them as 3-second views ÷ impressions, with 20–40% "generally solid"; Motion's FAQ instead defines thumbstop as 1-second views ÷ impressions. [P, vendor][U] — [Motion glossary](https://motionapp.com/library/glossary/thumbstop-rate-hook-rate); [Motion FAQ](https://motionapp.com/faq)
- **Google ABCDs (whole framework, not Shorts openings specifically):** Kantar scored ~11,000 ads with a custom model; Google states ABCD-following ads yield 30% lift in short-term sales likelihood and 17% in long-term brand contribution. These lifts do not isolate tight framing or openings. [M, platform-commissioned][U] — [Kantar](https://www.kantar.com/north-america/industries/technology-and-telecoms/validating-googles-abcd-framework-with-the-power-of-artificial-intelligence)
- **TikTok recall timing:** a search snippet attributes to TikTok Marketing Science ("Creative Guide: Driving Brand Equity", 2021) that 50% of recall impact occurs in the first 2 s and 50% of awareness impact in the first 2.5 s. My fetch of the insight page did not show this text. [snippet only, not confirmed on page] — [TikTok Creative Center insight](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/creativecenter/insight); [TikTok Works PDF](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/TikTok_Works_Recut_Remix_Reimagine.pdf) (not opened)
- **YouTube Shorts "viewed vs swiped away":** documented in Studio as the share of feed presentations where viewers watched rather than swiped; YouTube publishes no target rate or timing. [P][U] — [TechCrunch 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/25/youtube-demystifies-the-shorts-algorithm-views-and-answers-other-creator-questions/); [Fliki](https://fliki.ai/blog/youtube-shorts-algorithm)
- **Instagram skip rate (Aug 2025):** views skipped within the first 3 s. [P] — [Social Media Today via creatorflow](https://creatorflow.so/blog/visual-hooks/)
- **Emplifi feature correlations** (person, speech, text, vertical, loops, pacing) as listed in §§1–4. [M] — [Emplifi](https://emplifi.io/resources/facebook-reels-data/)
- Vendor thresholds such as "3-s retention under ~60% points to the visual hook" are presented without data. [O] — [BlitzCut](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/visual-hooks-instagram-reels)

### Inferences
- Each platform now exposes a first-seconds metric (YouTube viewed-vs-swiped, Instagram skip rate, ad hook rate), so the practical way to compare hook types for this pipeline is the channel's own A/B of first frames against those metrics, not external benchmarks.

### Gaps
- A third-party summary claimed Motion's 2026 report gives hit rates by opening type (offer-only 9.29%, confession 8.74%); the summary did not tie this to a page I could cite, so it is unverified.
- No public A/B or large-sample comparison of visual-hook types (mid-action vs result-first vs face, etc.) was found. Foreplay, Billo, Minta, OpusClip and CapCut data were not opened. TikTok Creative Center top-ads analyses by hook type were not available beyond four annotated ads.
- Most widely circulated numbers (47% value in first 3 s, 75% Reels sound-on, 90% of recall in 6 s, 67% show brand within 3 s) trace to vendor blogs; none verified.
