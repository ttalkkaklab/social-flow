# Verbal opening-line hook formulas for short-form video (first sentence / ~3 s)

Research date: 2026-11-18. Scope: the spoken/scripted first sentence of Shorts, Reels and TikTok.
Source quality note: almost all practitioner material reachable on the open web is secondary
(AI video-summary pages, tool-vendor blogs, book summaries). Where a template or claim comes only
from such a page it is labelled. "Folklore" = repeated advice with no measurement behind it.

## Q1. Catalog of verbal hook patterns (families, templates, real examples, fit and failure)

### Takeaway
About 50 distinct opening-line patterns can be credited to a named source. They fall into eight
families: curiosity gap, contrarian/pattern break, identity call-out, result/specificity/proof,
loss/warning, utility/list, story/experiment and offer (ad-only). The best-documented real
examples with named creators come from vidIQ's Shorts list. Only a few sources state when a
pattern backfires: the Runneth/Motion audience notes, Hormozi's notes on call-outs and one Korean
note that a contrarian line needs evidence within 5 s. Most other backfire notes below are
inferences and are marked as such.

### Cited Findings

Legend: **T** = fill-in template, **Ex** = real example (creator, reported views), **Fit** = when it
works, **Fails** = when it backfires. Mechanism labels are the researcher's classification unless a
source is given (see Q3).

#### Family A — Curiosity / information gap (mechanism: Loewenstein information gap)
1. **"Have you heard about…"**: T "Have you heard about [odd specific thing]?" Variants: "Did you know that…", "Have you ever wondered…", "Is it possible to…". Ex: Sean Andrew, "Have you heard about the world's loneliest toilet?" (16M+ views) — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/viral-video-hooks-youtube-shorts/)
2. **"Did you know…?" + object on screen**: T "Did you know this [everyday object] is [surprising property]?" Ex: Nas Daily, "Did you know this book is poisonous?" (31M views) — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/viral-video-hooks-youtube-shorts/)
3. **"Today years old"**: T "I was today years old when I found out [fact]." Ex: Bentellect explaining that "news" stands for Notable Events, Weather and Sport (6.6M views) — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/viral-video-hooks-youtube-shorts/). Fails: that NEWS etymology is a widely debunked backronym (researcher's note; not checked further). A fact-gap hook built on a false fact is a credibility risk.
4. **"They don't want you to know"** (forbidden secret): T "[Powerful party] doesn't want you to know [secret]." Ex: Techranium, Apple and an iPhone password hack (21M views) — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/viral-video-hooks-youtube-shorts/). Fails (inference): conspiratorial framing reads as clickbait when the reveal is ordinary.
5. **"You've never seen anything like this"**: T "You've never seen anything like this [object]." Ex: Mrwhosetheboss on GPods headphones (4M+ views) — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/viral-video-hooks-youtube-shorts/). Fit (inference): only when the picture really is novel in frame 1.
6. **Kallaway three-step hook (context lean-in → scroll-stop interjection → contrarian snapback)**: T "[Topic + why it's impressive, 1–2 sentences], **but get this,** [the thing everyone notices] is actually the least [X] part, because [real point]." Ex (Kallaway, Vegas Sphere, ~8M views): "the tech in the Vegas sphere is insane, biggest screen ever built, 20 times bigger than an IMAX, but get this, the screen is actually the least impressive part of the whole thing because the most impressive part is the audio." The interjection uses "but / however / yet / although" — [SozAI transcript of Kallaway, "How to Create Irresistible Hooks"](https://sozai.app/transcript/create-irresistible-hooks-blow-content/) (secondary transcript page); same framework summarized by [chaindesk summary](https://chaindesk.ai/tools/youtube-summarizer/how-to-create-irresistible-hooks-and-blow-up-your-content-LmXpbP7dD48)
7. **Plain curiosity tactic**: Fit: "top of funnel, content-led ads, audiences not yet emotionally invested" — [Runneth (formerly Motion) 33 hook tactics](https://runneth.com/library/hooks/tactics/)
8. **Sudden-change / loss opener (story cold open)**: Korean examples "자다 일어나니, 침대 옆이 텅 비어 있었다.", "오늘도 이별했다.", "단 하루 만에, 모든 걸 잃었다." These work because an unexplained change makes the viewer want the cause — [하이아웃풋클럽, 2025-07-11](https://blog.highoutputclub.com/instagram-reels-hook-tips/)
9. **Explainer (root cause)**: Fit: "Problem-Aware audiences who don't understand the root cause" — [Runneth](https://runneth.com/library/hooks/tactics/)
10. **Shock → intrigue → satisfy (Jenny Hoyos)**: The YouTube blog paraphrases her hook as three steps (shock, intrigue, satisfy). Direct quote: "I really do think you have one second to hook someone, especially on Shorts." — [YouTube Official Blog](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-shorts-deep-dive/)

#### Family B — Contrarian / pattern break (mechanism: expectation violation)
11. **Contrarian / myth flip**: T "[Common belief]? That's a lie." Korean Ex: "매일 올려야 팔로워가 는다? 그거 거짓말이에요." Fails: you must give the evidence within the next 5 seconds or trust collapses — [브런치 커리어 노트, 2026-07-17](https://brunch.co.kr/@@6knF/71). Fit: "educated, skeptical, or sophisticated audiences" — [Runneth](https://runneth.com/library/hooks/tactics/)
12. **Myth busting**: Fit: sophisticated, educated audiences — [Runneth](https://runneth.com/library/hooks/tactics/)
13. **"I never do THIS, and here's why"** (personal rule): T "I never [common behaviour], and here's the strange reason why." Ex: Dear Alyne in a Nas Daily Short, "I never buy silk, and this is the strange reason why." (33M+ views). Variants: "Why I'd never…", "Do's and don'ts of…" — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/viral-video-hooks-youtube-shorts/). This is the English analogue of Korean "~하지 마세요".
14. **Reverse psychology** ("don't watch this if…"): Fit: "ad-fatigued audiences" — [Runneth](https://runneth.com/library/hooks/tactics/)
15. **Shocking statement**: Fit: any awareness stage — [Runneth](https://runneth.com/library/hooks/tactics/)
16. **Confession**: Fit: "rebuilding trust, countering skepticism, polished or corporate categories" — [Runneth](https://runneth.com/library/hooks/tactics/)
17. **Question → reversal answer** (two-line flip): Korean Ex: "어? 저 어디서 본 적 없으세요?" / "당연히 없으시겠죠. 저는 무명 배우니까요." A short question raises curiosity and the reversed answer breaks the expectation — [하이아웃풋클럽](https://blog.highoutputclub.com/instagram-reels-hook-tips/)

#### Family C — Identity call-out (mechanism: self-relevance)
18. **Label**: T "Attention [group]…" — Hormozi verbal call-out taxonomy per [Margin Notes on $100M Leads](https://margin-notes-600.pages.dev/frameworks/verbal-callout-taxonomy) (secondary book notes)
19. **Yes-question**: T "Are you a [group] who [problem]?" Fails: a question nearly everyone answers yes to is not specific enough. Write it so only the target answers yes — [Margin Notes](https://margin-notes-600.pages.dev/frameworks/verbal-callout-taxonomy). Korean Ex (mirror the problem): "팔로워는 5천인데 좋아요는 30개도 안 찍히시나요?" — [브런치 커리어 노트](https://brunch.co.kr/@@6knF/71)
20. **If-then**: T "If you [situation], then this is for you." It works best when the audience is defined by a problem rather than a demographic — [Margin Notes](https://margin-notes-600.pages.dev/frameworks/verbal-callout-taxonomy). Fit: "segmented audiences with a clear, nameable condition" — [Runneth](https://runneth.com/library/hooks/tactics/)
21. **Demographic callout**: Fit: niche products, highly segmented audiences — [Runneth](https://runneth.com/library/hooks/tactics/)
22. **Direct address**: Fit: any awareness stage — [Runneth](https://runneth.com/library/hooks/tactics/). CapCut's example names the exact viewer type, e.g. a specific kind of editor — [CapCut](https://www.capcut.com/create/short-form-video-hooks)
23. **Relatability**: Fit: unaware and problem-aware audiences — [Runneth](https://runneth.com/library/hooks/tactics/)
24. **Benefit / pain-point first**: lead with the viewer's desire ("better sleep") before the topic ("magnesium"). Leading with the feature is the "disinterest" failure — [Kallaway via SozAI](https://sozai.app/transcript/create-irresistible-hooks-blow-content/)

#### Family D — Result, specificity and proof (mechanism: concreteness, credibility)
25. **Ridiculous result**: T "[Unbelievable outcome] in [short time]." Fails: it feels scammy without specifics, and round numbers look fabricated — [Margin Notes / $100M Leads notes](https://margin-notes-600.pages.dev/frameworks/verbal-callout-taxonomy)
26. **Result first (end state shown before process)**: Korean Ex "이 방법으로 팔로워 1만 명 만들었습니다". Cooking, home workout and interiors often flash the finished shot for 0.5 s first — [브런치 커리어 노트](https://brunch.co.kr/@@6knF/71). Korean ad Ex "3일 만에 피부가 달라졌어요" — [AMPM 마케팅 인사이드, 2025-08-14](https://inside.ampm.co.kr/insight/12120)
27. **Specific number**: Korean fragments "3초 만에", "97%가 모르는", "0원으로". The author claims odd numbers look less like ads (folklore, unmeasured) — [브런치 커리어 노트](https://brunch.co.kr/@@6knF/71). "월세 30% 줄이는 법", "임대료 200만 원 절감" — [AMPM](https://inside.ampm.co.kr/insight/12120)
28. **Statistic**: Fit: skeptical, analytical audiences — [Runneth](https://runneth.com/library/hooks/tactics/)
29. **Bold claim (+ "and I can prove it")**: T "[Bold claim]. And I can prove it." — [DEV Community (clamper_ai)](https://dev.to/clamper_ai/how-to-write-viral-hooks-for-short-form-video-with-examples-3pok). Fit: competitive categories where differentiation is hard — [Runneth](https://runneth.com/library/hooks/tactics/)
30. **Superlative personal verdict**: T "This is the best/most [X] I've ever used." Ex: Mrwhosetheboss on the most powerful charger he's handled (3.4M views) — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/viral-video-hooks-youtube-shorts/)
31. **Authority / credential prefix**: Korean Ex "10년 차 파티쉐가 추천하는 최고로 만들기 쉬운 디저트". The credential gives trust and "쉽다" invites imitation — [하이아웃풋클럽](https://blog.highoutputclub.com/instagram-reels-hook-tips/). Fit: health, wellness, skincare, finance — [Runneth](https://runneth.com/library/hooks/tactics/)
32. **Social proof**: Fit: product-aware audiences with hesitation — [Runneth](https://runneth.com/library/hooks/tactics/)
33. **Positioning in three words** (Hook Point): Brendan Kane's example is Netflix's "No Late Fees" against Blockbuster — [summaries.com, Hook Point](https://www.summaries.com/blog/hook-point) (book summary)

#### Family E — Loss, warning and fear of missing out (mechanism: loss aversion / negativity)
34. **Warning / "stop before you…"**: Korean Ex "잠깐! 이거 모르고 사면 손해입니다" — [AMPM](https://inside.ampm.co.kr/insight/12120). Fit: "Problem-Aware audiences about to make a mistake" — [Runneth](https://runneth.com/library/hooks/tactics/)
35. **Common-mistake loss**: Korean Ex "살 잘못 빼면 얼굴 10년은 늙어보이잖아". People react more to losses than gains — [하이아웃풋클럽](https://blog.highoutputclub.com/instagram-reels-hook-tips/). Fails (inference): exaggerated fear in health topics reads as anxiety-bait.
36. **FOMO**: Fit: product-aware audiences — [Runneth](https://runneth.com/library/hooks/tactics/)
37. **Urgency**: Fit: most-aware audiences — [Runneth](https://runneth.com/library/hooks/tactics/)

#### Family F — Utility, how-to and list (mechanism: clear promised value)
38. **"Try this to [goal]"**: Ex: KeeanSocial, "Try this to get more views on your next YouTube Short." (8M) — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/viral-video-hooks-youtube-shorts/)
39. **"How to create / How to become a…"**: Ex: Lydia Tomlinson, "how to create festive outfits" (1.3M); im_siowei, "how to become a mermaid in three steps" (22M) — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/viral-video-hooks-youtube-shorts/). Fit: problem- and solution-aware viewers actively seeking solutions — [Runneth "How To"](https://runneth.com/library/hooks/tactics/)
40. **"How to have the best [X] of your life"**: Ex: onlyjayus on the best sleep (reposted, 620K+) — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/viral-video-hooks-youtube-shorts/)
41. **"A simple hack to…"**: vidIQ's own variant "This could get your YouTube Shorts a lot more views". Fails: revealing the best part too early — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/viral-video-hooks-youtube-shorts/)
42. **Numbered list ("Three things I do before…", "Top 3…")**: Ex: Erika Kullberg's three pre-flight habits, plus her frequent-traveller credibility — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/viral-video-hooks-youtube-shorts/). Listicle/Reasons-why fit: educational top-of-funnel; solution-aware audiences needing logic — [Runneth](https://runneth.com/library/hooks/tactics/)
43. **Directive / imperative**: Fit: problem- and solution-aware audiences — [Runneth](https://runneth.com/library/hooks/tactics/)
44. **Problem question (education)**: Korean industry pattern for education/seminars: problem question → short answer hint → CTA — [AMPM](https://inside.ampm.co.kr/insight/12120)

#### Family G — Story, experiment, challenge and comparison
45. **"I'm trying this so you don't have to"** (proxy experiment): Ex: Sean Andrew trying Chinese water torture (6.9M). Variants: "Watch me…", "Today we're gonna see if it's possible to…" — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/viral-video-hooks-youtube-shorts/)
46. **"I asked Google…"**: Ex: Sean Andrew asks the longest jump in history, then tries to break it (478K) — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/viral-video-hooks-youtube-shorts/)
47. **"Questions I get asked a lot"**: Ex: Hwaniiee as a male nurse (8.8M) — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/viral-video-hooks-youtube-shorts/)
48. **Challenge / live situation**: Korean Ex "혹시 지금 영업 종료했나요?" (opening on a present situation, then showing the attempt) — [하이아웃풋클럽](https://blog.highoutputclub.com/instagram-reels-hook-tips/). Fit: competitive, achievement-oriented audiences — [Runneth](https://runneth.com/library/hooks/tactics/)
49. **X vs Y**: Ex: Mrwhosetheboss, "the thickest phone case or the thinnest phone case" (28M) — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/viral-video-hooks-youtube-shorts/). Contrast fit: price-sensitive audiences, upgrades — [Runneth](https://runneth.com/library/hooks/tactics/). Korean comparison Ex: "이 메뉴 판매량을 늘리고 싶나요?" — [하이아웃풋클럽](https://blog.highoutputclub.com/instagram-reels-hook-tips/)
50. **Cultural borrowing ("cult hopping")**: use a known brand, celebrity or cultural reference as a metaphor — [Kallaway via SozAI](https://sozai.app/transcript/create-irresistible-hooks-blow-content/). Ex (attributed to Hormozi, no video cited): "I learned this tactic from Eminem that he used in rap but actually makes sales way more effective." — [OpusClip blog](https://www.opus.pro/blog/3-easy-ways-to-make-viral-youtube-shorts-like-alex-hormozi) (unsourced attribution)
51. **Storytelling**: Fit: any awareness stage — [Runneth](https://runneth.com/library/hooks/tactics/)
52. **Visual/meme scene described in one line**: Korean Ex "불을 제대로 끄지 않고 일단 퇴근부터 하는 모습". One scene carries the risk without commentary and works best with a clear target — [하이아웃풋클럽](https://blog.highoutputclub.com/instagram-reels-hook-tips/)

#### Family H — Offer-led (ads only; awareness-stage specific)
53. **Call to action first / Offer only / Urgency**: most-aware audiences. **Price anchor**: mid-to-high ticket. **Risk reversal**: product-aware audiences hesitating. **Exclusivity**: premium/invite-only. **Aspirational**: unaware and problem-aware audiences. **Belief**: mission-driven brands — [Runneth](https://runneth.com/library/hooks/tactics/). Runneth gives no performance data for any tactic and only an unsourced "about three seconds to earn a pause".

### Inferences
- Families A, B and G share one device. The first sentence states a specific, visible fact or
  situation and leaves out its cause, reason or outcome. Kallaway's "but get this" and the Korean
  question→reversal pair are the same two-beat structure: set an expectation, then break it.
- vidIQ's high-view examples almost all put a concrete noun that can be pictured into the first
  clause ("loneliest toilet", "this book", "silk", "thickest phone case"). Specificity seems to be
  the common factor across families more than the family itself.
- Runneth's notes map patterns to awareness stage. For an organic story or explainer channel
  (unaware viewers), the relevant families are A, B, C (problem-defined), D and G. Family H belongs
  to ads.
- Patterns that promise a hidden truth (4, 25, 34) carry the highest mismatch risk. The video has
  to be able to show the payoff on screen.

### Gaps
- No primary written source from Hormozi was reachable. The call-out taxonomy (label, yes-question, if-then, ridiculous results) comes from third-party book notes. The "hook = 80% of ad effectiveness" figure appears in those notes without a source.
- Foreplay, Billo, Minta, Taplio, Jay Clouse, Colin and Samir and Creator Science were not reached within the tool budget. Their swipe files are not represented.
- Runneth's per-tactic pages (with examples) were not fetched. Only the index with audience-fit notes was read.
- Mechanism tags per pattern are the researcher's classification and are not experimentally validated per pattern.

## Q2. Practitioner taxonomies

### Takeaway
Kallaway teaches a three-step structure (context lean-in, scroll-stop interjection, contrarian
snapback) aligned across visual, on-screen text and speech. Jenny Hoyos stresses one second and
shock/intrigue/satisfy. Hormozi's call-out taxonomy targets self-selection. Runneth/Motion lists 33
tactics keyed to awareness stage. Brendan Kane's "Hook Point" is about positioning more than
first-line templates. MrBeast's guide concerns the first minute of long-form video and meeting
thumbnail expectations.

### Cited Findings
- Kallaway: the context lean-in states the topic clearly ("I need to be super clear about what the video is actually about") so viewers self-select. The interjection is "a single line meant to act like a stun gun". The snapback is "a sentence that goes in the opposite direction of the initial lean" — [SozAI transcript](https://sozai.app/transcript/create-irresistible-hooks-blow-content/)
- Kallaway's other tips: put 3–5 bold on-screen words that support the context lean, with just enough motion. Compress the time to value by giving a first payoff in or right after the hook. Use staccato short sentences in the hook and lengthen them later. Spoken context lean = 1–2 sentences. Short-form viewers decide in about 4 seconds — [SozAI transcript](https://sozai.app/transcript/create-irresistible-hooks-blow-content/)
- Kallaway says text + visual + speech together is far more powerful than speech alone. The listed mistakes map to delay (value buried), confusion (unexplained names/terms), irrelevance (topic or audience not stated) and disinterest (feature instead of benefit) — [SozAI transcript](https://sozai.app/transcript/create-irresistible-hooks-blow-content/) (the four-word labels are the researcher's mapping)
- Jenny Hoyos: "I really do think you have one second to hook someone, especially on Shorts." The blog paraphrases her formula as shock, intrigue, satisfy — [YouTube Official Blog](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-shorts-deep-dive/). Secondary summaries say she writes the hook and the last line first and foreshadows the payoff so viewers stay to the end — [Marketing Examined](https://marketing-examined.beehiiv.com/p/jenny-hoyos-shortform-video-playbook); [recapio digest](https://recapio.com/digest/how-she-makes-every-video-go-viral-jenny-hoyos-interview-by-amplify-views) (unverified against the video)
- Hormozi ($100M Leads, via notes): verbal call-outs = labels, yes-questions, if-then statements, ridiculous results. Test call-outs first because the same offer with different call-outs performs very differently — [Margin Notes](https://margin-notes-600.pages.dev/frameworks/verbal-callout-taxonomy)
- Runneth (formerly Motion) 33 tactics, listed under Q1, each mapped to an awareness stage, with no performance data — [Runneth](https://runneth.com/library/hooks/tactics/)
- vidIQ 18 Shorts hook ideas with creator examples, listed under Q1 — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/viral-video-hooks-youtube-shorts/)
- Brendan Kane, Hook Point: the premise is "3 seconds or less", with Netflix "No Late Fees" as the example. Summaries disagree on his audience typologies (Feelings/Facts/Fun versus six personality types) — [summaries.com](https://www.summaries.com/blog/hook-point); [Baylor Keller Center](https://kellercenter.hankamer.baylor.edu/news/story/2025/insider-hook-point-how-stand-out-3-second-world)
- MrBeast production guide (leaked Sept 2024; Passionfruit reported two former producers confirmed it): the creative process starts with title and thumbnail. The first minute "captures the viewer's attention and demonstrates that their expectations from the thumbnail will be met" (Willison's summary). Losing 21M of 60M clickers in the first minute is described as reasonably good — [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2024/Sep/15/how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/); provenance — [Passionfruit](https://passionfru.it/?p=81151)
- Paddy Galloway: secondary sources cite his analysis of ~3.3B Shorts finding the top performers at 70–90% "viewed vs swiped away". Sources disagree on the exact figure, and no direct hook instruction from him was found — search results such as [gofaceless.ai](https://www.gofaceless.ai/en/blog/youtube-shorts-retention-strategy) (secondary)

### Inferences
- Kallaway and Hoyos agree on structure. The first line sets an expectation (lean / shock), and
  the following beats deepen and then pay it off (snapback / satisfy).
- MrBeast's guidance is long-form, but the principle carries over to the first sentence of a Short:
  the opening confirms the promise the thumbnail, title or first frame made.

### Gaps
- No primary Kallaway text was available, only transcript pages. Exact definitions may differ from his own course.
- No Paddy Galloway primary source on hooks was found.

## Q3. Psychological mechanisms

### Takeaway
The information-gap theory (Loewenstein 1994) is the best-grounded explanation for curiosity
hooks. Curiosity needs some prior knowledge and a gap that looks closable. The Zeigarnik "memory
for unfinished tasks" effect, often cited by marketers, failed a 2025 meta-analysis. The more
robust finding is the tendency to resume interrupted tasks (Ovsiankina). For loss aversion and
negativity bias, self-relevance (cocktail-party effect) and concreteness, only practitioner
assertions were collected. Primary sources for those were not fetched.

### Cited Findings
- Loewenstein, "The Psychology of Curiosity: A Review and Reinterpretation", *Psychological Bulletin* 116(1), 75–98 (1994): curiosity is a deprivation arising from a perceived gap in knowledge — [stafforini.com entry](https://stafforini.com/works/loewenstein-1994-psychology-curiosity-review/)
- Curiosity needs some initial knowledge, and priming with intriguing but incomplete information triggers it — [PBS NOVA](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/nova/article/how-to-stimulate-curiosity/). The gap must seem closable, and curiosity rises as partial information brings people closer to the answer — [Information Gap Theory summary](https://concepts.dsebastien.net/concept/information-gap-theory/) (secondary)
- Zeigarnik: the 2025 meta-analysis (Ghibellini & Meier, DOI 10.1057/s41599-025-05000-w) found no memory advantage for unfinished tasks, but a general tendency to resume them. "The Zeigarnik effect lacks universal validity" — [RePEc record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/pal/palcom/v12y2025i1d10.1057_s41599-025-05000-w.html); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeigarnik_effect). Secondary figures (59 publications, recall ratio 0.99, 67% resumption) were not verified against the paper.
- Loss/negativity: "people are more sensitive to losses than to gains" is used as the rationale for negative hooks — [하이아웃풋클럽](https://blog.highoutputclub.com/instagram-reels-hook-tips/) (practitioner assertion; no primary study fetched)
- Contrarian lines stop people because information that the common sense they hold may be wrong makes them pause reflexively — [브런치 커리어 노트](https://brunch.co.kr/@@6knF/71) (practitioner assertion)
- A mirrored problem leads the viewer to judge unconsciously that "this account has the answer" — [브런치 커리어 노트](https://brunch.co.kr/@@6knF/71) (practitioner assertion)
- A sustained over-optimised 3-second hook without value "trains audiences to click, feel disappointed, and disengage" — summary of Kane's Hook Point in [search results](https://www.summaries.com/blog/hook-point) (secondary)

### Inferences
- Mapping (researcher's): Family A → information gap. B → expectation violation / pattern interrupt.
  C → self-relevance. D → concreteness and credibility. E → loss aversion / negativity bias.
  F → explicit value promise. G → narrative transport + information gap.
- Loewenstein's "some prior knowledge" condition explains Kallaway's context-lean-in step. Without
  stating the topic first, the gap is not perceived. It also explains why unexplained jargon
  (Kallaway's "confusion") kills a hook.
- Because the Zeigarnik memory effect does not replicate, "open loop" claims should rest on
  information-gap curiosity and resumption, not on a memory advantage.

### Gaps
- Primary sources for negativity bias (e.g., Baumeister et al. 2001), loss aversion (Kahneman & Tversky), the cocktail-party/own-name effect (Moray 1959) and the concreteness effect were not fetched in this pass. They are cited here only as standard concepts.
- No controlled study comparing hook families on short-form retention was found. All performance figures are creator anecdotes or vendor claims.

## Q4. Hook–payoff alignment, length, speed and first words

### Takeaway
Practitioners converge on these rules. Speak the promise within the first second or so. Cut
greetings, logos and "hey guys". Keep the spoken hook to one idea of about 5–10 words (on-screen
text 3–7 words). Align picture, on-screen text and speech. Pay off what the hook promised, and
keep the best part from being given away too early. The numeric thresholds (VVSA 70%, retention
gains from cutting greetings) are unverified folklore.

### Cited Findings
- Length: 3–7 words of on-screen text and 5–10 words spoken. If the hook needs a second sentence to make sense, simplify it. Viewers decide in about 1 second. Skip filler greetings. Don't tease a payoff the video doesn't deliver. Compare viewed-vs-swiped-away against your own recent Shorts, not a universal benchmark — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/viral-video-hooks-youtube-shorts/)
- Kallaway: 3–5 on-screen words and a 1–2 sentence spoken context; staccato sentences; front-load value — [SozAI transcript](https://sozai.app/transcript/create-irresistible-hooks-blow-content/)
- Cut the logo sting, "hey guys" and channel intro. Context can come at second 5, not second 1. Test by cutting the first 2–3 seconds and starting where content begins — aggregated in [gofaceless.ai](https://www.gofaceless.ai/en/blog/youtube-shorts-retention-strategy) and similar blogs (secondary). A claimed 5–10 point retention gain from cutting the greeting has no data behind it (folklore).
- First spoken word within 0.5 s of the start; no music intro — vendor blog advice surfaced in search ([virvid.ai](https://virvid.ai/blog/first-3-seconds-hook-faceless-shorts-2026)) (folklore, unmeasured)
- Viewed-vs-swiped-away targets of 70%+ or 75–80% vary by source. Bytecap says there is no official universal threshold — [Bytecap](https://www.bytecap.io/research/youtube-shorts-retention-benchmarks)
- Payoff alignment: a contrarian claim must be backed within about 5 s — [브런치 커리어 노트](https://brunch.co.kr/@@6knF/71). MrBeast's first minute confirms the thumbnail's promise — [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2024/Sep/15/how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/). Hoyos foreshadows the payoff from the hook (secondary) — [Marketing Examined](https://marketing-examined.beehiiv.com/p/jenny-hoyos-shortform-video-playbook)
- A Korean counter-view warns against obsessing over the first 3 seconds and argues the middle matters more for retention. Published 3-second drop-off figures (65%, 40–60%) vary widely with no official basis — summarized from [AMPM](https://inside.ampm.co.kr/insight/12120) and Korean search results (e.g. [i-boss](https://www.i-boss.co.kr/ab-6141-73342); page returned 403, so only the search snippet was read)
- AMPM's unsourced figures (70% of ad performance decided in the first 3 s, +20% retention with faces or eye contact, an anonymous beauty brand going from 42%→65% retention) cannot be verified — [AMPM](https://inside.ampm.co.kr/insight/12120)
- Hoyos checks scripts at a fifth-grade reading level and cites a 90% retention benchmark. Both come from secondary summaries only — [search summaries](https://recapio.com/digest/how-she-makes-every-video-go-viral-jenny-hoyos-interview-by-amplify-views) (unverified)

### Inferences
- With Korean TTS at a typical ~5–7 syllables/s (researcher's assumption, not sourced here), a
  5–10-word English-equivalent hook is roughly one Korean sentence of 15–20 syllables, which fits
  inside 3 s.
- Starting mid-sentence or mid-action is the speech form of a cold open. It removes the greeting
  slot entirely. Sources recommend it, and none measure it.
- A hook that the video cannot prove on screen fails under all three families of guidance
  (vidIQ's "don't tease", the Korean 5-second evidence rule and MrBeast's expectation matching).

### Gaps
- No source found gives a measured optimal speaking rate (words per minute) for hooks.
- No platform-official (YouTube/TikTok/Meta) document was found that defines a clickbait penalty for hook–payoff mismatch. The mechanism described is indirect, through swipe-away and retention metrics.

## Q5. Korean-language hook phrasing

### Takeaway
Korean practitioner sources use the same families: loss/warning ("이거 모르고 사면 손해입니다"),
result first ("3일 만에 피부가 달라졌어요"), specific numbers ("97%가 모르는", "0원으로"),
contrarian ("…? 그거 거짓말이에요"), mirrored problem questions, sudden-change story lines and
question→reversal pairs. The commonly cited forms "~하지 마세요" and "3초만 투자하세요" were not found
in a fetched source in this pass.

### Cited Findings
- (Korean) "잠깐! 이거 모르고 사면 손해입니다", "3일 만에 피부가 달라졌어요", "월세 30% 줄이는 법", "임대료 200만 원 절감". By industry: beauty uses before/after; food uses the finished close-up; education opens with a problem question; real estate and B2B open with a shocking number — [AMPM 마케팅 인사이드, 한규태, 2025-08-14](https://inside.ampm.co.kr/insight/12120)
- (Korean) "이 방법으로 팔로워 1만 명 만들었습니다", "3초 만에", "97%가 모르는", "0원으로", "매일 올려야 팔로워가 는다? 그거 거짓말이에요.", "팔로워는 5천인데 좋아요는 30개도 안 찍히시나요?" — [브런치 커리어 노트, 2026-07-17](https://brunch.co.kr/@@6knF/71)
- (Korean) "자다 일어나니, 침대 옆이 텅 비어 있었다.", "오늘도 이별했다.", "단 하루 만에, 모든 걸 잃었다.", "10년 차 파티쉐가 추천하는 최고로 만들기 쉬운 디저트", "어? 저 어디서 본 적 없으세요?" / "당연히 없으시겠죠. 저는 무명 배우니까요.", "혹시 지금 영업 종료했나요?", "살 잘못 빼면 얼굴 10년은 늙어보이잖아", "이 메뉴 판매량을 늘리고 싶나요?" — [하이아웃풋클럽, 2025-07-11](https://blog.highoutputclub.com/instagram-reels-hook-tips/)

### Inferences
- The Korean "~하지 마세요" form corresponds to the English "I never do THIS" and warning tactics
  (Q1 #13, #34). "이거 모르면 손해" corresponds to the loss/warning family.
- For a story-led channel, the 하이아웃풋클럽 story lines (sudden change, question→reversal) are the
  closest Korean models. They state an event and hold back its cause, without marketing vocabulary.

### Gaps
- "~하지 마세요", "3초만 투자하세요" and similar stock phrases are folklore here. No fetched Korean source used them verbatim with an example. A follow-up search on Naver blogs and brunch would be needed.
