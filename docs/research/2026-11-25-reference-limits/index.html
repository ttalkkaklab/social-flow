Reference limits for MiniMax H3, LTX-2.5 MSR and Seedance

Read from the upstream sources on 25 November 2026. Each number carries a mark saying where it comes from.

One MiniMax H3 ref2va request takes nine pictures, three voice samples and three videos, twelve files in all. One LTX-2.5 request with the Licon MSR adapter takes five reference pictures and two voice samples. One Seedance request takes nine images, three videos and three audio clips on the 2.0 series, and thirty, ten and ten on 2.5. None of these models sets a number of characters. A character usually costs a picture slot, and backgrounds and shot pictures draw on the same slots, so that budget is the working limit.

A shot picture means something different in each model. H3 reads it as one more numbered reference with no time attached. LTX-2.5 takes a still at a frame index through its own image conditioning, outside the five MSR slots. No upstream source documents how such stills behave across cuts, or shows them used together with MSR. Seedance numbers a shot picture as one more reference, as H3 does. Its 2.5 guide adds a keyframe instruction that the output follows "relatively strictly". An exact first or last frame is a separate mode on Seedance and cannot be mixed with references.

How to read the evidence

Five kinds of support appear in the tables. A wrapper or gateway figure is none of them. Those are listed separately in Wrapper figures against upstream. Seedance has no public weights or code, so its figures rest on documentation alone.

  • author stated by the model's author in a card, paper or guide
  • code checked by the author's released code
  • default a value an example workflow ships with
  • maintainer said by a maintainer in an issue thread
  • unverified not found in any source read

MiniMax H3 ref2va

H3 is published as two transformer partitions. The ref2va partition conditions on an ordered list of pictures, videos and audio clips. The limits below are the ones MiniMax documents for the released checkpoint. The diffusers pipeline checks them, and its own comment says they "bound nothing but this block's own validation".

Nine pictures, however you spend them

A character usually costs one picture, and two when a face and an outfit are sent separately. Every shot picture and background comes out of the same nine. Two things loosen the count. One picture can hold more than one character: the guide says "one reference asset may provide multiple subjects". A reference video can define a character as well, as the guide's worked example does for both of its people.

  • character identity
  • shot composition
  • background or prop
  • unused

Who can speak

The prompt guide binds a voice sample to a speaker in a sentence: <Audio 1> is the voice-timbre reference for <Subject 1> (S1). A speaker without a sample is allowed. The guide's own three-shot example gives (S1) a sample and lets (S2) speak "in a casual young male voice" from the description alone. So three standalone clips can pin three speakers, and further speakers get whatever voice the description produces. The guide also counts a reference video's enabled soundtrack as an audio asset. Whether that pins a fourth voice is not shown anywhere.

How a reference gets its number

The diffusers pipeline numbers references per kind, in the order of the list. A video that carries sound takes an audio number first and then its video number.

The prompt guide says something else: "An ordinary reference video does not create <Audio N> merely because the file contains sound." The pipeline labels every soundtracked video. The guide does not say what makes a soundtrack "enabled", so check the numbering whenever a reference video has sound.

LTX-2.5 with Licon MSR

MSR, Multiple Subject Reference, is an adapter from Licon Studio for LTX-2.5. The author's workflow runs it on the distilled transformer. Each reference picture is encoded as its own group of tokens with a learned slot number. V2 of the adapter adds voice references. Lightricks' own multi-subject method is different: one composite sheet of characters, props and locations on a black background.

Five slots

  • character or object picture
  • background
  • unused

Five is where the training data stops

The slot number goes through a small network that accepts any integer, so nothing in the architecture stops at five. The paper says this "is not evidence of reliable generation with an unlimited number of references". The training set explains the number.

Three speakers

A user reported on the Lightricks repository that one or two referenced characters work well and that a third speaking character makes identities blend and lines land on the wrong person. A Lightricks member answered on 4 November 2026 and the issue was closed as not planned:

Keeping identity and dialogue bound correctly across three or more referenced speakers is beyond what the current model handles reliably; two subjects is the practical sweet spot today. … we suggest keeping two speaking subjects per shot and covering the third with cuts or reverse shots.

The paper lists two related failures: subjects with similar looks or clothes can blend, and appearance can drift when a character turns or returns after a cut.

How a reference enters the model

  1. One still per slot. A multi-view collage in one picture is still one reference.

  2. The still is repeated for 25 or 33 frames and encoded by the video VAE on its own. That makes 4 or 5 latent frames per reference.

  3. The slot's learned embedding is added to every token of that group.

  4. The group joins the sequence as clean tokens with a small negative time offset. For three references at 25 fps the offsets are −0.12, −0.08 and −0.04 seconds.

  5. After sampling, the node chain crops the reference tokens off before decoding.

Voice references use a different arrangement: one 5-second window per picture slot, laid out before the clip with a 0.04-second margin. A missing sample leaves its window empty and does not renumber the others.

Shot stills are a different input

The images list in the supplied request is LTX-2.5's own image conditioning. It does not use an MSR slot and it is not limited by the five. The two inputs come from different surfaces. The Licon workflow has no frame-conditioning node beside its reference guide, and the LTX-2 pipelines have no MSR. Sending both in one request is the in-house backend's composition, and no upstream example shows it.

The image budget

Characters, settings, props and shot pictures are all reference images and are numbered in one sequence.

  • character
  • setting or prop
  • shot picture or keyframe
  • unused

How many subjects the guides recommend

Both prompt guides give figures well under the input limits. They are advice, and no request is refused for going past them. The 2.0 guide counts reference people. The 2.5 guide counts subjects. A subject is not only a person: the guide lists "characters, animals, props, locations, and more".

  • "generally produce better results"
  • "stability may decrease"
  • outside the guides' ranges
  • With more than five subjects the 2.5 guide calls single-view images "generally more stable" and asks for extra views as separate images.
  • For a subject's audio or video reference the 2.5 guide says 5 to 10 seconds "generally works better".
  • Past four reference people the 2.0 guide expects "an incorrect number of people" or duplicated characters. Its own case put in 8 reference characters and got 9 people. The workaround it gives is to compose groups of at most four into images first and generate the video from those images.
  • The 2.0 guide recommends 4 to 5 assets in a request: "1-2 character images (facial close-up / full body) + 1 scene image + 1 camera movement video + 1 audio clip". It adds: "It is not recommended to use the full asset limit."
  • For character images the 2.0 guide asks for "independent single-person photos" and says three-view or multi-view sheets are "not recommended", because they can make the model draw the same character twice. The 2.5 guide supports multi-view images.

A reference audio clip can carry a voice timbre, music or spoken content. The prompt says which.

The shared length is the tighter limit. At the 5 to 10 seconds the 2.5 guide recommends per subject, 30 seconds holds three to six voices. Ten voices need clips of 3 seconds or less. On the 2.0 series three clips must average 5 seconds. These figures are our arithmetic from the documented limits.

A speaker needs no clip. Both guides show dialogue between characters defined by images alone. The largest such prompt, with five named speakers across eight images, is a troubleshooting case in the 2.5 guide: "characters may be mismatched with their reference images, voice timbres, or order of appearance". Its remedy is to upload assets in the order the subjects first appear and to renumber the prompt to match. The 2.0 guide has its own entry for a generated voice that "differs significantly from the reference voice", and its fix is to describe the voice in words beside the audio reference.

Real faces

Neither series accepts a directly uploaded image or video that contains a real human face. The documents give three routes: a preset digital character, an authorised real-person asset, and a face-containing output generated under the same account in the last 30 days. The first two are passed as asset://<ASSET_ID> in place of a URL. The prompt still refers to that asset as Image n, because an asset ID in prompt text is not supported.

Fitting a scene into one request

These checks follow from the limits above. Passing them means the request is accepted. It does not mean the result holds every identity and voice.

MiniMax H3 ref2va

  1. Count the pictures: identity, background, prop and shot. Nine or fewer?

    If not, put two characters in one picture, drop outfit or shot pictures, or split the scene.

  2. Count the standalone voice clips. Three or fewer?

    If not, describe the extra voices in words or split the scene.

  3. Twelve files or fewer, with at least one picture or video?

  4. Does a reference video carry sound?

    If so, renumber the <Audio N> labels.

  5. Set a frame count on 17n + 5 between 124 and 345.

  6. Write each cut in the prompt. The shot pictures carry no time.

LTX-2.5 with MSR

  1. Count the reference pictures. Four or fewer, plus at most one background?

    If not, merge views into one sheet or split the scene.

  2. Do more than two characters speak?

    If so, keep two speakers per request and cover the third in another shot.

  3. Put the two voiced characters in pic1 and pic2. Keep each sample's useful part inside 5 seconds.

  4. Keep to 2 to 4 shots and name each transition in the prompt.

  5. Set a frame count on 8n + 1.

  6. Stills after frame 0 are optional and unsupported by any document. Inspect the render before relying on them.

Seedance 2.0 and 2.5

  1. Count the images: characters, settings, props and shot pictures. Within 9 on the 2.0 series, 30 on 2.5 or 10 on flash?

    If not, drop extra views or shot pictures, or split the scene.

  2. More than four reference people on 2.0? On 2.5, more than eight subjects by image or five by audio or video?

    If so, expect retries. On 2.0 compose groups of up to four into images first. On 2.5 use single-view images above five subjects.

  3. Count the voice clips and add up their length: 3 clips in 15 seconds on 2.0, 10 clips in 30 seconds on 2.5.

    On 2.0 an audio clip also needs an image or a video in the request.

  4. Does any image or video show a real person's face?

    If so, use an asset ID or an output generated by the same account in the last 30 days.

  5. Must the first frame match a picture exactly?

    If so, use the first-frame mode and give up references. On 2.5, keyframe references come close while keeping them.

  6. Order the assets by first appearance and map each character to its images and audio in the prompt.

  7. Write each cut as Shot N. Add whole-second timestamps on 2.5 only.

The plugin reaches Seedance through its own ModelArk adapter. Its limits of 9 images and 3 audio clips on the 2.0 series, 30 and 10 on 2.5 and 10 and 5 on 2.5 flash agree with the documentation. Reported by the gateway's own notes and not measured for this study: on production LTX renders, cuts requested at frames 48 and 49 landed at 37 and 44, and only the opening still was reproduced. That matches the absence of upstream support for stills at cuts. It covers a small number of runs.

Open questions

  • H3 with all nine pictures, or with three pinned voices. The English examples read stop at four pictures and two speakers.
  • Whether a soundtracked reference video should get an <Audio N> label, and whether that soundtrack can pin a voice beyond the three standalone clips. The pipeline and the prompt guide disagree on the label.
  • Whether H3's limits are a property of the weights. The pipeline's comment says a fine-tune could raise them. MiniMax's own inference code is not public.
  • MSR with four or five slots filled. The training data covers it and no example shows it.
  • The hidden third audio input of the MSR node, and whether the adapter was trained with it.
  • Which LTX version the adapter was trained on. The paper's table says "LTX (22B)" and the card says LTX-2.5.
  • Where MSR reference tokens sit. The paper and the weights' metadata say they are prepended. The node appends them as keyframes. The transformer code was not read.
  • LTX-2.5 frame stills together with MSR references, and stills at shot boundaries in a multi-shot clip.
  • How many characters Seedance keeps distinct near its image limit. The guides' figures, four reference people on 2.0 and 1 to 8 subjects by image on 2.5, come with no measurements.
  • Whether the Seedance 2.0 series holds several speakers bound to separate clips. Its tutorial documents the sentence for one and shows no multi-speaker example.
  • Whether the keyframe instruction works on Seedance 2.0. It is documented for 2.5 only.
  • Recommended subject counts for Seedance 2.5 flash, which has a third of the images.
  • The limits of the Dreamina app, a different surface from the API. A search summary gave 12 assets for 2.0 there, and that page was not opened.
