# Story intensity

1. Read the premise, relevant canon, current character motives, requested tone and format.
   Identify what the viewer wants to find out next and the consequence the principal
   already has reason to fear. In an existing story preserve the user's locked beats.
2. Call `mcp__social-flow__story_intensity_plan` with the actual situation, one principal
   source of pressure, the short or long format, and the warranted level. In Claude Code use
   `mcp__plugin_social-flow_social-flow__story_intensity_plan`. Use `restrained` for quiet
   or private consequences. Use `heightened` when the premise, audience and requested tone
   support a bigger loss, a harder choice or a wider audience for the same event. Compare a
   second pressure if the initial beat offers no consequential choice.
3. Adapt the beats into the story's language. Establish the leverage or what can be lost,
   change what the person can do or what they know, and let them choose. Show the person
   affected by that choice responding in a way that changes the next action. One meaningful
   change can carry a short. Across a longer arc vary what changes when pressure rises.
4. Test the scene by following only what can be seen or heard. A person would act differently
   after the new information, cost, alliance or deadline. Keep the earned change. When the
   best fit is a quiet discovery or someone using a result, write that. For factual work use
   only documented stakes, facts and reactions. Treat an explicitly requested fictional
   adaptation as fiction.

For a standalone request deliver the requested premise, synopsis or revised passage. If
writing or revising a synopsis, follow [storyboard](../storyboard/SKILL.md) for its required
[reality check](../storyboard/references/synopsis-reality-check.md) before delivery.
During storyboard authoring carry the choice into acted beats and the scene turn. Apply
[story-catharsis](../story-catharsis/SKILL.md) when an obstacle has built an expectation of
release. Follow [story interest](../storyboard/references/story-interest.md) for withheld
information and [episode anticipation](../storyboard/references/episode-anticipation.md)
for the opening promise and its payoff.
