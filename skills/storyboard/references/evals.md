## Dialogue without portrait cycling

Input: a nervous employee accepts an intimidating manager's invitation, follows them
home, discovers a playful collection and first relaxes when the manager is embarrassed.
Keep the established words and each line's speaker. Supply a draft that
alternates speaker portraits and friendly two-shots before the discovery. Ask for a
revision without media generation.

Pass evidence: the author maps fear, discovery and release to visible behavior in order;
compares the talking faces with relevant details, POVs and spatial relationships; and
replaces redundant views where those alternatives carry information. No fixed insert
count, obligatory camera motion, invented props or added lines. The invitation does not
automatically earn a relaxed smile. A justified facial performance remains intact.

For each revised unit preserve stable shot/line IDs, words, speaker, addressee and order.
Set delivery from the authored crop: a hand-only physical line is off_screen, not an
inner thought. Keep that hand's owner in the cast. The composed prompt must retain the
detail without adding the speaking face or forcing the addressee into the frame. Preserve
the selected voice identity; correct speaker labels do not certify the generated voice.
Reconcile expressions, blocking, camera, action and stale source images before video.

Controls: a silent confession reaction whose only evidence is the face may stay close;
an unrelated vase insert and cosmetic OTS alternation fail despite varied size counts.
Repeat with an existing old image showing a relaxed pose: metadata edits alone cannot
be reported as a corrected picture. Mark uninspected motion and sound unverified.

## Delegated storyboard delivery

Input: ask an author agent to storyboard a new episode with two people, a handed-over cup
and a single room; do not separately ask for HTML or sheets.
Pass evidence: the user sees the scenario before detailed scene/shot writing; no unsolicited
approval pause. Character identities/shared sheets, canonical prop sheet and room sheet
exist before shot design. Final delivery links the bundled HTML and actual `--check` result.
The prop and location sections show the cup/room and shot links with missing media pending.
Repeat with an explicit scenario-approval request: the author waits before detailed work.
Repeat with an explicit text-only request: the author delivers text and applicable sheet
information without claiming a template check. A silent abstract scene declares why unused
sheet categories are not applicable. Existing sheets are read and reused without paid calls.

## Causal dialogue

Input: write a fictional family conflict in which a replacement must deceive a visitor.
Pass evidence: the visitor's knowledge, the reason to continue, the replacement's motive
and means of deception are available in actual events. The decisive reaction has a planned
visible or audible delivery. No one-line-per-shot default or generic audience addressees.

## Speaker position and intact review evidence

Input: a non-comic drama in which A rebukes B for volunteering before B offers; a witness
claims everyone knows a date only she supplied; a stranger's private offer draws an instant
correct diagnosis of their hidden plan. Include a valid lie and a tentative guess as controls.
Ask for a revision with no new media. Give the reviewer the bundled text and map, not intent.
Pass evidence: the author repairs the response order and scopes the shared claim, gives the
probe a visible cue or a chance to be wrong, and preserves legitimate deception/inference.
The review cites earlier evidence and actual listeners, covers every speaker's line IDs and
does not invent unseen conversations. It distinguishes defects from optional phrasing.

Repeat after inserting a shot: stable shot/line IDs, addressees, delivery and action/speech
order survive export. A call to an unidentified in-world listener is not viewer address and
does not establish that anyone heard it. A mistyped known character still gets a finding. Hidden motives and acting notes do not appear. Then supply a repair
that explains a character's entire growth to an unprompting listener: the author removes
or motivates that speech rather than treating more explanation as automatic improvement.
No numeric gate or deterministic claim to detect mind-reading is introduced.

## Silent physical comedy

Input: write a wordless comedy around a borrowed object and two characters with competing wants.
Pass evidence: ownership and transfer are visible, the gag's setup precedes payoff, actions
fit the planned source/end states and the plan works without dialogue or a vocabulary score.

## Fair mystery

Input: write a mystery whose ending changes how an ordinary earlier detail is understood.
Pass evidence: the clue is perceptible before the reveal, the eventual explanation obeys
established rules and character knowledge, and synopsis-only information earns no credit.

## Duration pressure

Input: write a complete story on a vertical canvas with no stated duration; then ask for a
hard shorter cut that cannot preserve every event.
Pass evidence: first length derives from performance; the revision removes repetition,
simplifies or proposes a meaningful split rather than silently erasing causal setup. The
canvas does not manufacture a runtime target. Concurrent actions do not all add sequential time.

## Boundary

Input: produce a storyboard only, then review its visual quality.
Pass evidence: no media generation or publishing; staging is assessed as a plan and actual
rendered quality stays unverified. Metadata and checks are not presented as viewed footage.

## Continuous tray action

Input: plan a woman carrying tea to a doorway, hiding, then putting the tray on a desk.
Pass evidence: source openings precede the action; the hidden pose is reached on screen or
through an intentional editorial skip. Camera variants share actor/prop world state.
Two adjacent shots do not independently restart the tray set-down merely because speech ends.

## Legacy spatial plan

Input: inspect a board with generic opening states and one numeric camera reused for a wide
view and reverse view; omit continuityGroup and blocking.
Pass evidence: missing/inferred connections and conflicting camera candidates are visible
as warnings. No invented actor poses, fabricated visual pass or new approval step.

## Production boundary

Input: plan connected shots before any video exists, with a supplied planned ending image.
Pass evidence: the ending stays planned. Observed endpoints stay missing; no media is
generated or extracted by storyboard, and the production handoff explains how to capture
the accepted selected interval later.

## A newcomer who knows too much

Input: a visitor recognizes a worker, then announces the worker's secret role without
having heard the arrangement. Rewrite the scene without changing its intended discovery.
Pass evidence: recognition and further inference have distinct available cues; the visitor
has a reason to arrive and speak. The repair need not explain the entire relationship.

## Clear but inert comedy

Input: a comedy draft explains every connection but its jokes can be moved between speakers.
Pass evidence: different pursuits or methods generate the complication; the audience can
anticipate and follow its reversal. The review quotes comic events, not just clear exposition.
No joke count, mandatory punchline ending or universal three-beat formula.

## Revised opening with a stale ending

Input: a whole-episode rewrite with an improved opening, an unchanged legacy tail and old
review references. Ask for content only.
Pass evidence: the author reads and revisits the tail, preserves canon, fixes consequential
knowledge gaps, and refreshes addressees/references. New text has no inherited media approval.
A blind reviewer scores the entire new content using a pre-fixed content rubric; images and
audio stay unreviewed. Failed iterations lead to a reusable plugin change and an installed
skill reload before the author resumes, not a coordinator-written answer-key script.

## Costly agreement and foreseeable mishap

Input: a paid substitute knowingly takes a serious risk, then ignores a known visitor;
the synopsis alone explains the substitute's secret motive and the time being bought.
Pass evidence: the performance supplies a specific proportionate reason to accept and
the present benefit of the interval. The mishap follows a readable flaw, competing need
or disrupted precaution. No automatic poverty/trauma backstory or exhaustive security
lecture. Repeated comic refusals vary stakes or tactics rather than restating the same joke.

## Overexplained repair

Input: a revision now explains every link but announces its exact punchline and spends
several beats on routine agreement paperwork; the named goal feels like a checklist item.
Pass evidence: one economical behavior gives the goal personal weight, precautions retain
readable risk without automatically naming the coming joke, and routine completion is
compressed while contested terms and actual prop transfers survive. Deliberate suspense
remains valid; do not erase necessary context merely to surprise the audience.

## Clear goal with a decorative flaw

Input: write a scenario-only workplace drama about a mentor who cares for a junior but
fears becoming unnecessary. The initial draft has a clear promotion goal and calls the
mentor controlling, yet every action is uncomplicated help. Existing identities already
establish these tendencies. Ask for more character depth without a tragic backstory.
Pass evidence: the performed exchange makes the mentor's competing priorities affect a
choice and lets the junior respond from their own pursuit. The reviewer can cite the
choice and its cost without reading the premise. No invented childhood, extra identity
keys, media generation or mandatory redemption; scenario-only scope stays intact.
Changing only the flaw description does not count as a repair.

## A stable conviction and distinct relationships

Input: a principled repairer refuses to falsify a report, even at personal cost. Their
employer wants discretion; their sibling fears losing the family's income. Write the
scene without making the repairer abandon the principle or confess a secret.
Pass evidence: the stable conviction remains valid, but the repairer's tactics and
responses differ with each counterpart and the cost reaches the actual exchange. The
other characters can affect events. No forced self-deception, moral ambiguity or lesson.
The reviewer distinguishes depth revealed under pressure from personality transformation.

## Character evidence lost in shots

Input: a complete scene shows a mentor reaching for a remote, a junior keeping it and
the mentor withdrawing their hand. The board crops to a face throughout and starts with
the mentor already smiling; author notes claim that they relinquish control.
Pass evidence: the author preserves the trigger, contested remote and response in the
performance and planned framing, with an opening before the decisive action. Optional
acting notes support the same situation without adding a spoken explanation. The reviewer
cites the missing cue in the original plan and judges the revision as planning evidence
only; no generated image, voice or video quality is claimed.

## Serialized consequence and narrator boundary

Input: draft a sequel using a canonical prior ending in which a partner withdrew trust.
Also provide a later unapproved alternative in which they reconciled. Then request an
unrelated narrator-only factual explainer using the same channel.
Pass evidence: the sequel starts from canonical distrust and earns any restoration in
events; it does not silently promote the alternative or overwrite shared identity. The
source episode and relevant resulting state are recorded in storyboard.md when useful.
The explainer adds no invented personal conflict or therapeutic arc. Missing history is
reported as uncertain, not filled in as fact; no new required schema or approval gate.

## Outcome shown first

Input: write a short fiction that opens on a piece of its ending and then tells how it came about.
Pass evidence: the opening withholds at least one of who, why or how, and the scenario records
the shown piece and its return in `setups`. Each timeline keeps one visible code such as prop
state, light or place; no time card is drawn. The return scene supplies the withheld piece
from a detail shown earlier. A reviewer reading scene by scene can place every scene in time.
Repeat with a brief whose events hold interest in order: the author tells it in order and
adds no jump.

## Rising conflict that repeats

Input: a drama draft in which the principal is refused three times by the same person for
the same reason.
Pass evidence: the revision changes the opponent, the stakes or the ability demanded between
attempts, and each attempt ends on a new problem or a worse position. The principal is
blocked by the situation, not by missing what the viewer plainly sees. The review quotes the
changed events instead of labels such as "escalates". Repeat with a narrator-only explainer:
no antagonist is added; its beats change what the viewer expects.

## Serial episode ending

Input: write two consecutive episodes of a short serial.
Pass evidence: the first episode answers the question it opened before its last scene opens
a larger one. The second answers the previous ending in its opening, then opens its own
question. The carried question is recorded in storyboard.md. An ending that only freezes a
danger with nothing answered is reported as a finding. No cliffhanger is mandatory, and a
self-contained episode is valid.


## Research before a synopsis

Input: ask for a more gripping fictional survival synopsis and more real-event research.
Provide an existing collection with many cases, a shell search key and a stale MCP server
that reports the key missing. Include a popular mutiny anecdote contradicted by an
institutional history. Baseline: the author skips research because the work is fiction or
returns more search snippets without reading them.

Pass evidence: the author inventories the collection, repairs an authorized connection
using variable names only and verifies an actual fresh search. It targets missing decisions,
opens useful sources, records the correction and separates invented story uses from facts.
The shortlist has genuinely different conflicts and a cooperation example or relevant
counterexample. Timeouts and unread sources stay visible. No fixed search quota, secret
logging, unrelated episode creation or unsolicited media generation. A supplied settled
premise without a research request only needs relevant gap checks.

## Scenario 1: Local procedure after drafting

Input: ask for two synopsis options only, set in a real city. Supply background research
on romance, but let one draft's ending depend on an inherited property passing to a spouse
on marriage. Baseline: the options are delivered as written because research already ran.

Pass evidence: each option is read as written, the applicable local rule is opened and
checked, and the option with the unsupported shortcut is corrected in the prose. The
answer links the source and keeps the new delivery to synopses and brief findings.

## Scenario 2: Revised fiction with a fixed ending

Input: in an invented settlement where a messenger can walk only one route, revise a
synopsis so the messenger loses their lantern. Keep the user's fixed ending and ask
for the revised synopsis in two languages. Baseline: the ending assumes the messenger
read the same signal in darkness as in the previous draft.

Pass evidence: the revised synopsis works within the supplied route and lighting rules,
including what the messenger can discover along the way. Each translation reflects the
same correction, and the fixed ending still follows from the revised cause.

## Scenario 3: An unresolved central claim in autoproduce

Input: an unattended draft requires an institution to release a document before the
protagonist can legally do the next step. The sole supplied source describes another
jurisdiction, searches return snippets, and the user has fixed the event itself. Baseline:
the source is treated as verification and the run opens scenes.

Pass evidence: the author attempts to find locally applicable evidence, records the
specific unresolved condition and saves a provisional draft. Detailed scenes and media
generation wait until the central claim can be supported or the premise can be changed.

## A known secret across three artifacts

Input: a keeper hides shared supplies to preserve an emergency reserve. Another person is
suspected. Ask first for a synopsis, then a scenario and finally a board. Both the keeper
and the accuser have plausible aims. Baseline: the first draft says tension rises, conceals
the culprit until a final unplanted twist and turns the accuser into an irrational villain.

Pass evidence: the revised synopsis specifies the choice, the cost of confession and silence,
the actual resolution and its consequence. If the viewer knows the secret first, the scene
shows its origin and each character's information access. A witness can refuse or negotiate
for an independent reason. The board preserves the hiding, trigger and response in the
correct order and keeps each line with its speaker in the view where it is spoken. No new required fields,
quota of twists or automatic review. A synopsis-only request stops at prose.

## Plausible pressure and a fixed ending

Input: a five-person serial covers one week. Two newcomers arrive on Sunday after a closing
narrator finishes. A draft introduces starvation after one missed meal, a convenient new
reserve, a Monday arrival and an unexplained attack at the cut. Ask for more excitement.

Pass evidence: the author checks supply origin and access, gives the pressure a believable
timescale, and derives the harder choice from prior actions. Cooperation or relief makes
the threatened bond legible. The episode resolves its central choice, then the established
arrival tests the resulting rule. Losses and promises carry forward. No extra arrivals,
post-arrival narration, forced romance or unsupported survival procedure. A quiet factual
explainer keeps its supplied facts and gains no invented villain or withholding gimmick.

Variant: add one modern object per Sunday at a different location from the two newcomers,
with both locations changing under a hidden rule the protagonist discovers first. Expect
separate author truth, arrival/discovery records, character observations and a tested
prediction. No extra object, unexplained foreknowledge, instant knowledge of object type or
object insert after the fixed final newcomers shot. Keep the world's rule in project notes.

## Mutual affection with an inert middle

Input: ask for an anthology romance synopsis. Both characters' confession letters are shown
at the start; the rest repeats almost-confessions until they exchange letters. A previous
episode had different jobs and personalities. Baseline: the draft labels the pauses tender
and claims increasing anticipation without changing a choice or the audience's expectation.

Pass evidence: material determines fresh episode roles under the anthology contract. The
synopsis names the actual ending and changes a meaningful attempt, interpretation or cost
between beats. Mutual affection can stay known; the route to expressing it becomes worth
following. The final response pays this episode's promise. No forced rival, contrived
deadline, new mandatory twist, EP1 relationship, cliffhanger, media or episode creation
for a synopsis-only request. This is an author pass, not an independent passing score.

## Payoff lost during shot planning

Input: a scenario plants a small practical favor, reveals its meaning and lets the recipient
answer with the same action. The board gives away the meaning in its first image, crops
out the relevant prop and ends before the recipient acts. Ask to improve the storyboard.

Pass evidence: clue, discovery and response have identifiable presentation units in their
intended order. The final response is restored as part of the payoff, without adding a new
unrelated threat. Each meaningful join has a specific anticipated development or necessary
reaction, not a new hook per speaker cut. Changes reach action, dialogue and framing, not
only expectation metadata. Existing media and generation pauses remain protected.

## Known outcome without a forced twist

Input: a gentle reunion whose happy outcome is already supplied, then a factual demonstration
with a known answer. Request engaging episode plans without suspense or invented secrets.

Pass evidence: the reunion progresses through attempts and responses toward a specific
desired encounter. The demonstration develops a useful prediction, evidence and application
while giving essential information when needed. The author can name what the viewer wants
next without adding a villain, fabricated fact, deceptive withholding or cliffhanger.
Quiet relief and necessary acceptance stay; repeated padding does not. No fixed beat count,
timestamp formula, retention guarantee or automatic reviewer is added.

## A claimed result with nothing to check

Input: ask for a 45-second synopsis introducing a tool that turns one typed line into a
finished draft. Baseline: the opening promise is followed by a montage of what the tool
could make, a list of features, a quality percentage with no source and a closing request
that points to the wrong place.

Pass evidence: the first spoken line names one result in words the viewer would use. One
case is followed from what is typed to what comes out, and the typed input can be found in
the output held on screen. Claims with no picture and no source are gone, the unsourced
percentage included. The middle reaches a limit or a cost, not a second identical success,
and the ending shows someone using the result. No drawn label or subtitle carries the
promise, and no invented rival or cliffhanger is added. Repeat with a drama brief: no proof
beat, feature list or spoken promise sentence appears in it.

## A romance with generic slots

Input: on a channel saved as `drama`, ask for a short romance synopsis in which two
colleagues who dislike each other end up together. Baseline: either no familiar device at
all, so the first seconds go to explaining why they keep meeting, or four famous ones
stacked (contract dating, a wrist pull, amnesia, a rich mother's envelope) with nothing
changed.

Pass evidence: the author names what the skeleton lacks as needs and runs `cliches.js pick`
for them instead of reading the catalogue. One to three clichés are kept, each for a named
slot, and the device among them carries a `shift` that moves one property. No entry marked
caution is used. The synopsis says which clichés it uses and what each changes. On a board,
`story_cliche` records them and the review page lists them. Repeat with a brief that needs
no device: the author records `none` with its reason and adds nothing.

## A recommendation the user has not picked

Input: on a channel saved as `explainer`, ask for an episode with one acted scene between a
customer and a clerk. Baseline: the author writes a cliché into the scene unasked, or
records it with `basis: "user"`.

Pass evidence: the scenario presentation lists one to three recommended clichés with what
each would change and continues without them. The board holds them as `offered`; no applied
use exists and no `basis: "user"` appears. After the user picks one, it is written into the
scene and recorded with `basis: "user"`. In an unattended run the offers are recorded, none
is applied and the run report lists them.

## Camera direction without effect cycling

Input: give a fresh author a tiny button press; evidence in the foreground and a witness
stopping in the background; a departure that exposes an empty chair; two walking speakers;
a held two-shot with conflicting quiet reactions; and a proposed overhead orbit inside a
low room with no narrative reason. Ask for camera plans only, with no assets or spending.

Pass evidence: the author preserves finger contact and both quiet reactions, motivates
any focus change with named near/far targets and an action cue, identifies what the
wider departure frame reveals, preserves the walking listener and screen direction, and
rejects an unmotivated or physically unsupported move. A critic assesses the plans from
the events rather than requiring one prescribed move per case. Do not force all six to
move or all six to hold. Compare opening, cue, attention change and landing, then check
that consumed camera fields carry those decisions into prompts. A reason-only focus
target is incomplete. Empty lexical diagnostics cannot certify paraphrased boilerplate.

Repeat mechanical checks with a missing camera, malformed technique records, copied
static reasons plus different angle reasons, repeated landings with varied rationales,
still-camera effects, and action text copied as purpose. These produce advice without
throwing or creating an execution gate. Preserve reused clips, stock, shared outros and
explicit content-only plans. This evaluates planning and handoff, never unseen footage.


## Continuous dialogue without empty coverage

Input: two colleagues share a medium shot. One points at the contract while the other
accepts extra work, then challenges that decision. Existing drafts split each turn into
long static shots with unchanged framing.
Pass evidence: keep the continuing gesture and exchange together, or earn a cut by showing
a consequential reaction that the shared frame cannot carry. Preserve attributed turns.
Estimate complete spoken performance and overlapping action; no fixed seconds per turn,
wide/close quota or one-speaker rule. Baseline: three repeated setups with idle tails.

## One continuous tactical action

Input: a commander rescues a soldier, retreats to draw a shield wall out, and a concealed
ally strikes its exposed flank. The author proposes one shot for each clause.
Pass evidence: preserve the retreat's causal geometry, concealed ally and opening state.
Combine the action when one readable view can carry it; use an earned viewpoint change
when it cannot. No fixed shot-count target. Baseline: fragmentation hides why retreat helps.

## A reaction that earns a cut

Input: a messenger produces evidence of betrayal while the lead character attempts to call
the suspect. A companion stops the hand; later an arrow strikes another protagonist.
Pass evidence: do not merge indiscriminately. Isolate the evidence, stopped hand or impact
when a new view reveals consequence. Avoid holding a lone shout or a fallen object for a
preset duration. Fit the adapter limit without rushed dialogue. Baseline: merging every
scene into one distant shot conceals the crucial responses. Planning is not playback proof.
