# Dialogue that changes the situation

Use for dialogue-led scenes and revisions of existing stories. These are editorial
questions, not required fields, joke quotas or execution gates. Keep the user's genre:
clarity is not a demand to explain every inference or make every character prudent.

## A line needs a present-tense reason

Before a consequential line, find its trigger in the performed events. What did this
speaker just see, hear or remember from an established relationship? Who hears the line,
and what does the speaker try to do with it: stall, warn, bargain, conceal, invite, test,
refuse? A spontaneous emotional reaction is also a reason. If only the writer needs the
line to introduce a person or deliver a twist, repair the event before polishing wording.

Separate recognition from inference. Knowing someone's face or job does not grant
knowledge of their secret, current role or intent. A newcomer needs only enough business,
relationship or visible evidence to make the consequential inference legible. Show the
cue at the entrance when that is sufficient; do not insert a biography or explain a joke.
Check who can hear a warning: announcing the secret beside its target can contradict
the speaker's immediate purpose unless the slip is deliberate and has a consequence.

Let the listener's response affect the next move. A return question, silence, blocked
path or misunderstood gesture may carry the connection. Do not allocate one isolated
sentence to every cut or set `to: audience` for an ordinary exchange. Read speech aloud
without author notes: does it sound like a person handling this situation now?

For a costly agreement, knowing the risk and naming a payment are not always enough.
What makes acceptance worth this person's risk now? Supply a proportionate, specific
stake, competing obligation, meaningful gain or bounded commitment in the performed
scene. Hidden canon cannot supply the motive. Do not invent a tragic backstory for every
choice; one concrete pressure or telling choice may suffice. A contract records terms,
not physical protection. Distinguish what is promised from what the parties can control.
When buying time drives a plan, show what that interval enables and why a simpler exit
would not serve the same purpose. The audience needs its present benefit, not the full
solution to future episodes. An arbitrary deadline repeated in dialogue adds no reason.

A named goal is not its emotional weight. Let a small present action, a sacrifice already
made or the way the person treats something show what the gain means to them. Do not add
an explanatory life story when an economical behavior can carry it. Keep this distinct
from declaring the choice irrational: a legible risky bargain may need resonance, not
another justification or a guarantee that nothing bad can happen.

## Make the situation funny, not only its labels

For comedy, identify a particular mismatch between people's pursuits or methods. One
tries to protect status; another treats the same problem as paid work; a third simply
wants to finish a task. Show their methods collide. A solution may create the next
obstacle, or an earlier ordinary task may return at the worst possible moment. Coincidence
can start trouble; it must not silently supply indispensable knowledge or solve the ending.

Give the audience enough to anticipate the mishap, then vary or overturn the expected
response. Preserve the reaction that lets the reversal register. A joke can be a look,
object, delay or failed tactic; no minimum number of spoken jokes or rule of three applies.
Avoid interchangeable witty lines. Ask whether the line could move to any other character
unchanged, and whether removing the gag changes the negotiation or relationship at all.
Some comic asides are welcome; an entire scene of detachable quips is not escalation.

Do not repair confusion with a lecture. Prefer a cue that both advances the business and
reveals information: delivery instructions, an interrupted greeting, a refusal or a prop
someone tries to hide. Do not make the victim of a serious threat the comic target by
accident. Genre and the channel's boundaries decide what can be played lightly.

Do not make otherwise alert characters forget an obvious risk merely to deliver a gag.
If they foresee it, show their reasonable precaution, competing priority or deliberate
gamble, then let an established action, constraint or character choice disrupt it. A foolish choice can
still work when the flaw and consequence are readable. Do not explain every possible
safeguard; retain only the one whose failure changes the scene. On repeated comic beats,
ask what changes: pressure, tactic, status, information or consequence. Cut repetitions
that only reconfirm the same relationship; preserve a purposeful running joke's variation.

After a clarity repair, run an economy pass. Retain the information once it is delivered
by an event; remove nearby dialogue that merely announces the exact coming mishap. Choose
deliberate suspense or surprise rather than hiding necessary context. A precaution can
show that the character sees the danger without naming the punchline. Compress routine
completion steps after a negotiation has changed the relationship: keep contested terms
and consequential handling, not every duplicate signature or confirmation. A prop gesture
must convey a concrete change in custody, leverage, attention or feeling; decorative hand
movements do not make exposition acted. Check that cuts preserve the necessary causal cue.

## Revisit the whole story after a local repair

For a rewrite, read all existing scenes, including a tail marked unchanged or pending.
State the invariant premise/canon separately from changeable dialogue, order and blocking.
Preserve the requested story; a cleaner unrelated plot is not a successful revision.
For a narrow edit, trace downstream dependencies without silently expanding its scope.
For an authorized whole-story rewrite, actually re-author weak downstream scenes.

After changes, read the continuous performance through the ending. Check entrants,
knowledge, listener responses, prop states and elapsed time across every join, not only
new shots. Refresh references and addressees from the new order; earlier review scores
do not apply. Keep old media and approvals with their original revision. A content-only
rewrite has no new media, paid calls, inherited audio receipts or fabricated timing.

## Blind reading and requested improvement loops

Give a fresh reader only observable actions and attributed words, with useful place/time
headings. Ask what happened and why, what each person could know, what the viewer expects
next, and where a comic expectation is set up and changed. Record uncertainties before
showing intentions or canon. A reader may infer ordinary behavior; essential unseen
facts cannot be supplied by the evaluator to rescue the story.

When content-only comedy scoring is requested and no rubric is supplied, agree/fix the
scope before writing: causality/comprehension 25; knowledge/motivation 20; situational
comedy 20; setup/payoff 20; dialogue/rhythm 15. Cite specific events for credit and
deductions. Fun means readable pursuit, anticipation, escalation, surprise and character
response, not a promise of measured laughter or retention. Images, audio and actual
performance stay unreviewed. Other genres keep an appropriate requested rubric.

For a user-requested plugin improvement loop: preserve every draft and verdict. Extract
the reusable cause from each failed review, revise the plugin, update/verify the intended
local installation, and have the author reload it before the next draft. The coordinator
does not patch the benchmark script; the reviewer never edits it. Use a fresh reviewer
without prior scores or the target, retain the frozen rubric, then compare prior findings
after their independent read. Scores need not rise. Refresh the whole revised story,
including untouched scenes. A threshold is only this user's task criterion.
